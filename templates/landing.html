What one search does

Searches eleven ways at once

Semantic search over every claim and paragraph, claim-only search, keyword and phrase, CPC classification, the citation and family graph, query-by-example, cross-lingual English and German, your own document's passages, your drawings against our figure index, and a live fan-out to eight external patent APIs.

Reads the shortlist end to end

Around 600 candidates are screened, then roughly 180 are read in full: every claim, every paragraph. Each feature of your invention gets a verdict, a verbatim quote and its location, and every "discloses" is checked by a second pass told to refute it.

Ranks on evidence, not similarity

The number on each card is what that reference was measured to disclose, weighted by how rare the disclosure is. A reference we could not read never outranks one we quoted, and every claim on the page can be traced to a sentence in a document.

The full pipeline, step by step

Built for the decision, not the demo

  • Prior-art dating in code. Novelty includes secret art under EPC Art. 54(3); inventive step is public art only. Dates are never left to a model.
  • One card per patent family. The same invention filed in six countries is one result.
  • Element by reference chart with a quote and a location in every filled cell.
  • Best reference per feature, rarest first: the single-feature document an inventive-step attack is built from.
  • Exports to PDF, DOCX, XLSX, Markdown and an IDS-style citation listing.
  • Drafting workspace: turn a search into a draft specification with figures.

Honest about its limits

Every report and every export states the scope: which offices are indexed, how deep the text goes for each, what is missing, and the measured reliability of the chart. Those numbers come from evaluation runs and are not adjusted to look better.

The corpus is deep in vacuum gripping, lifting and handling and wide but shallower elsewhere, and the page says so on every search. A query outside the indexed field is detected and routed to the external patent APIs instead of being answered with the nearest local neighbours.

The full scope statement and the measured numbers

And then it writes the application

Prior art is only useful if it changes what you file. From a finished search, or from nothing but a description of your invention, a drafting agent writes the specification, the drawings and the claims — reading every reference first, and telling you which feature carries each independent claim clear of which document.

You revise it by talking to it: correct a fact, upload another reference, ask why a limitation is there, ask for a different claim strategy. Every iteration is checked by a separate reviewer before you see it — reference numerals against the drawings and the text, claims against what was actually disclosed, and every citation against a publication that really exists. What comes out is a filing package, with the things only a person can do listed rather than quietly ticked off.
