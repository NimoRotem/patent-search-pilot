In the following description, the terms upstream and downstream refer to the direction of travel of the carrier liquid from the inlet toward the outlet, and the terms axial and radial refer to the axis along which the piston slides.

FIG. 1 shows an inline liquid metering valve 10 having a cylindrical housing 12 with an inlet 14 at one axial end and an outlet 16 at the opposite axial end, a metering sleeve 18 fixed within the housing 12, a cartridge 64 retained in the housing 12 by a threaded end cap 66, and a transparent window 52 in the housing 12.

The inline liquid metering valve 10 is connected in a line that already carries a flowing carrier liquid. Carrier liquid enters at the inlet 14, passes along the housing 12, and leaves at the outlet 16. That flow is the main flow into which each dose of concentrate is delivered, and it is also the flow that rinses the valve. The metering sleeve 18 is spaced radially inward from the inner surface of the housing 12.

FIG. 2 shows the metering sleeve 18 defining a dose chamber 20, a concentrate port 22 opening into the dose chamber 20 and a carrier-liquid admission port 24 opening into the dose chamber 20. FIG. 2 further shows an upstream sealing land 30 and a downstream sealing land 32 joined by a reduced-diameter waist 34 in the fill position, and a one-way outlet valve 38.

The dose chamber 20 is cylindrical. The concentrate port 22 is a radial port through the wall of the metering sleeve 18 and is connected in use to a concentrate source. The carrier-liquid admission port 24 passes through the wall of the metering sleeve 18 at an axial position downstream of the concentrate port 22, and admits carrier liquid to the dose chamber 20. The metering sleeve 18 further defines a flush bypass 26 that opens downstream of the dose chamber 20.

A piston 28 is axially slidable in the dose chamber 20 between a fill position and a discharge position. The upstream sealing land 30 and the downstream sealing land 32 are axially spaced from one another on the piston 28 and are joined by the reduced-diameter waist 34. Each sealing land bears on the wall of the dose chamber 20, while the waist 34 stands clear of that wall. A piston rod 36 extends from the piston 28 along the axis of the dose chamber 20 and is arranged to be driven through a stroke and a return stroke. The one-way outlet valve 38 is arranged to pass liquid from the dose chamber 20 toward the outlet 16 and to prevent flow in the opposite direction.

In the fill position the waist 34 connects the concentrate port 22 to the dose chamber 20, and the upstream sealing land 30 and the downstream sealing land 32 isolate the carrier-liquid admission port 24 from the concentrate port 22. Concentrate accordingly passes from the concentrate source through the concentrate port 22 into the dose chamber 20 to form a measured volume of concentrate, the extent of which is fixed by the travel then permitted to the piston 28. Throughout the fill position the carrier-liquid path is closed to the concentrate, because the downstream sealing land 32 overlies the carrier-liquid admission port 24 while the upstream sealing land 30 closes the dose chamber 20 on the other side of the waist 34.

Movement of the piston 28 toward the discharge position closes the concentrate port 22 before the downstream sealing land 32 uncovers the carrier-liquid admission port 24. Only after the concentrate port 22 has been closed does continued travel of the piston 28 carry the downstream sealing land 32 clear of the carrier-liquid admission port 24. Carrier liquid then enters through the carrier-liquid admission port 24 behind the measured volume of concentrate in the dose chamber 20 and expels the measured volume through the one-way outlet valve 38 toward the outlet 16, where it joins the main flow.

The axial ordering of the faces of the upstream sealing land 30 and the downstream sealing land 32 relative to the edges of the concentrate port 22 and the carrier-liquid admission port 24 is chosen so that the concentrate port 22 is closed at every position of the piston 28 at which the carrier-liquid admission port 24 is open. There is accordingly no position of the piston 28 at which the concentrate source communicates directly with the carrier-liquid path. The separation of the two liquids is a property of the geometry of the piston 28 and the metering sleeve 18. It does not depend on the order in which external commands are given, on any control signal, or on the correct operation of a separately actuated shut-off element. The port that must close does so at an earlier point on the same continuous path of travel than the point at which the other port opens.

FIG. 3 shows the piston 28 during the return stroke toward the fill position, with a flexible lip 56, a follower pin 44 holding a flush poppet 58 open, an annular rinse channel 60, two radial rinse openings 62, the concentrate port 22, and the flush bypass 26. The follower pin 44 is carried by the piston rod 36.

The flexible lip 56 is carried on the upstream sealing land 30 and bears on the wall of the dose chamber 20. As the return stroke begins, the flexible lip 56 wipes the wall of the dose chamber 20.

The annular rinse channel 60 lies between an outer surface of the metering sleeve 18 and an inner surface of the housing 12, and is supplied with carrier liquid from the inlet 14. The two radial rinse openings 62 pass through the wall of the metering sleeve 18 and connect the annular rinse channel 60 to the dose chamber 20. One of the two radial rinse openings 62 is directed across the concentrate port 22 and the other is directed across the one-way outlet valve 38.

The flush poppet 58 is normally closed and is connected to the flush bypass 26. Near the end of the return stroke, the follower pin 44 reaches the flush poppet 58 and opens it. Carrier liquid then flows from the inlet 14 through the annular rinse channel 60, through the two radial rinse openings 62 across the concentrate port 22 and the one-way outlet valve 38, and through the flush bypass 26 to the outlet 16. The rinse liquid therefore reaches the outlet 16, as the dose does, and is carried away in the main flow.

The flush poppet 58 closes before the waist 34 reconnects the concentrate port 22 to the dose chamber 20. Throughout the interval in which the annular rinse channel 60 communicates with the flush bypass 26, the waist 34 is out of register with the concentrate port 22, so that the waist 34 does not connect the concentrate port 22 to the dose chamber 20. Only when the flush poppet 58 has closed does the continuing return of the piston 28 bring the waist 34 back into register with the concentrate port 22, at which point the dose chamber 20 is charged again for the next dose.

Because the follower pin 44 that opens the flush poppet 58 is carried by the piston rod 36, the rinse occurs once for every dose and at the same point in every cycle. It is not a separate operation, it needs no command, and it cannot be omitted while the valve continues to be used. The rinse also occurs before the waist 34 has reconnected the concentrate port 22 to the dose chamber 20 and after the wall of the dose chamber 20 has been wiped.

FIG. 4 shows an index collar 40 having a helical cam groove 42 engaged by the follower pin 44 carried by the piston rod 36, three selectable axial stop faces 46, and three dose setting numerals 54. FIG. 4 further shows a spring-biased detent 48 engaging one of three detent recesses 50.

The index collar 40 surrounds the metering sleeve 18. Each full stroke of the piston 28 draws the follower pin 44 along the helical cam groove 42 and turns the index collar 40 by one increment, so that the index collar 40 advances by itself as the valve is used and needs no separate indexing drive.

The three axial stop faces 46 are carried by the index collar 40 at different axial positions. The stop face presented by the current increment of rotation limits the travel of the piston 28 and thereby sets the dose volume, so that the three axial stop faces 46 correspond to three dose volumes. The spring-biased detent 48 engages one of the three detent recesses 50 to retain the selected setting until the index collar 40 is next indexed.

The transparent window 52 in the housing 12 shows one of the three dose setting numerals 54 carried by the index collar 40. The dose setting numeral presented at the transparent window 52 is the one associated with the axial stop face then limiting the travel of the piston 28, so that the dose setting is mechanically visible without electronics. What is seen at the transparent window 52 is the position of the index collar 40 itself.

The cartridge 64 contains the piston 28, the metering sleeve 18, the one-way outlet valve 38 and the flush poppet 58, and is removable from the housing 12 as a unit. Removing the threaded end cap 66 withdraws the cartridge 64 from the housing 12 without disturbing the connections at the inlet 14 and the outlet 16, so that the cartridge 64 is replaced as a unit while the housing 12 remains in the line.

In use, carrier liquid flows through the housing 12 from the inlet 14 to the outlet 16. With the piston 28 in the fill position, concentrate is admitted through the concentrate port 22 and past the waist 34 into the dose chamber 20 to form a measured volume, the upstream sealing land 30 and the downstream sealing land 32 isolating the carrier-liquid admission port 24 from the concentrate port 22. The piston 28 is then advanced toward the discharge position so as to close the concentrate port 22 and thereafter to uncover the carrier-liquid admission port 24 with the downstream sealing land 32. Carrier liquid is admitted through the carrier-liquid admission port 24 behind the measured volume and expels the measured volume through the one-way outlet valve 38 toward the outlet 16. The extent of the travel available to the piston 28, and therefore the measured volume, is fixed by the selected one of the three axial stop faces 46.

The piston 28 is then returned toward the fill position. During the return, the flexible lip 56 on the upstream sealing land 30 wipes the wall of the dose chamber 20, and the follower pin 44 carried by the piston rod 36 opens the normally closed flush poppet 58. Carrier liquid flows through the annular rinse channel 60 between the metering sleeve 18 and the housing 12, through the two radial rinse openings 62 in the metering sleeve 18 across the concentrate port 22 and the one-way outlet valve 38, and through the flush bypass 26 that opens downstream of the dose chamber 20 to the outlet 16. The flush poppet 58 is closed before the waist 34 reconnects the concentrate port 22 to the dose chamber 20. Over the same full stroke, the follower pin 44 traveling in the helical cam groove 42 turns the index collar 40 by one increment, the spring-biased detent 48 enters the next of the three detent recesses 50, and the dose setting numeral then presented at the transparent window 52 shows the setting that will govern the next dose. The valve is thereby left charged, rinsed and set, ready for the following cycle.
