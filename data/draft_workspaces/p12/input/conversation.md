### USER

Here is my invention.

A reusable inline liquid metering valve delivers a repeatable dose of concentrate into a flowing carrier liquid and automatically rinses the surfaces that contacted the concentrate after each dose. The valve has a cylindrical housing with an inlet at one axial end and an outlet at the opposite axial end. A metering sleeve is fixed inside the housing. The sleeve defines a cylindrical dose chamber, a radial concentrate port, a carrier-liquid admission port, and a flush bypass that opens downstream of the dose chamber.

A piston slides axially in the dose chamber between a fill position and a discharge position. The piston has two axially spaced sealing lands joined by a reduced-diameter waist. In the fill position, the waist connects the concentrate port to the dose chamber while both lands isolate the carrier-liquid path. Movement to the discharge position closes the concentrate port before the downstream land uncovers the carrier-liquid admission port. Carrier liquid then enters behind the measured volume and expels that volume through a one-way outlet valve into the main flow. The ordering of the port edges and lands prevents the concentrate source from communicating directly with the carrier-liquid inlet.

An index collar surrounds the metering sleeve and has a helical cam groove engaged by a follower pin carried by the piston rod. Each full piston stroke turns the index collar by one increment. Three selectable axial stop faces on the collar set three dose volumes by limiting piston travel. A spring-biased detent engages one of three recesses to retain the selected setting. A transparent window in the housing shows one of three reference numerals carried on the collar, so the dose setting is mechanically visible without electronics.

When the piston returns toward the fill position, a flexible lip on the upstream sealing land first wipes the dose-chamber wall. Near the end of the return stroke, the follower pin opens a normally closed flush poppet connected to the flush bypass. Carrier liquid flows through an annular rinse channel between the sleeve and housing, through two radial rinse openings directed across the concentrate port and the one-way outlet valve, and then through the flush bypass to the outlet. The flush poppet closes before the waist reconnects the concentrate port. A replaceable cartridge contains the piston, metering sleeve, one-way outlet valve, and flush poppet and is retained by a threaded end cap. Removing the end cap withdraws the cartridge without disturbing the housing connections. An apparatus claim and a method claim should cover the ordered isolation, metering, discharge, and automatic rinse sequence.
