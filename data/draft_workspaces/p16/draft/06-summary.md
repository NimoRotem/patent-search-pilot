A reusable split pipe clamp is disclosed in which the ring that drives the jaws is itself split, one segment traveling with each half of the frame, and is made into a working ring by the act of closing the frame.

The clamp has a first frame half and a second frame half joined at a hinge end by a hinge and joined at a latch end by a releasable latch, so that the frame halves can be swung apart, placed around a pipe, and closed again. Three jaw carriages are distributed circumferentially about the closed frame. Each jaw carriage is slidable in a radial guide of the frame and carries a jaw pad that is mounted on the jaw carriage for pivoting movement and that presents a concave bearing surface to the pipe. Because each jaw pad pivots, it bears on the pipe without requiring a tool specific pipe diameter.

A cam ring is retained in an annular guide of the frame. The cam ring is segmented. A first cam-ring segment is carried by the first frame half and a second cam-ring segment is carried by the second frame half, so each segment stays with its own frame half when the clamp is opened. The first cam-ring segment and the second cam-ring segment have complementary drive faces at the hinge end and complementary drive faces at the latch end. Closing the frame halves brings those complementary drive faces into engagement and couples the cam-ring segments so that they move together as one cam ring about the pipe.

Each jaw carriage carries a follower that is received in a respective oblique slot of the cam ring. The latch carries a wedge. Moving the latch drives the wedge against a ring drive face of the cam ring and rotates the coupled cam ring through a limited stroke about the pipe. Each oblique slot converts that common rotation into inward travel of its own jaw carriage, so that all of the jaw carriages advance together through the same inward travel. A carriage spring biases each jaw carriage outward, so that releasing the latch retracts the jaw carriages and lifts the jaw pads off the pipe before the frame halves are opened.

The latch has a first position in which it holds the frame halves around the pipe without clamping and a second, over-center position in which it drives the cam ring and clamps the pipe. The two positions separate the act of capturing the pipe from the act of loading the jaws.

A force-limiting spring stack lies in the drive path between a latch handle of the latch and the wedge. Once all of the jaw pads have contacted the pipe, further movement of the latch handle toward the over-center position compresses the force-limiting spring stack rather than forcing one jaw pad farther than the others. The latch travel that remains after contact is therefore taken up by the force-limiting spring stack.

An indicator pin is visible on the latch and moves relative to a fixed edge of a latch housing of the latch when the force-limiting spring stack enters a predetermined compression range, giving the operator a mechanical report that the clamp is loaded within that range. The indicator reports clamp loading and does not measure pipe diameter.

The frame may carry a mounting boss outside the annular guide for supporting a sensor, a tool or a conduit. The mounting boss does not participate in synchronizing the jaw carriages.

A method of clamping a pipe and a method of releasing the clamp are also disclosed, in which the frame halves are closed about the pipe to couple the cam-ring segments, the latch is moved to the first position and then to the over-center position to rotate the coupled cam ring, and the latch travel that remains after the jaw pads seat is taken up by the force-limiting spring stack.
