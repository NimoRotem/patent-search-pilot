A split pipe clamp is disclosed in which the element that advances the gripping jaws is divided with the clamp body but is coupled by the act of closing the clamp into a single element that moves as one ring, so that one latch motion advances every jaw together.

In one aspect, a split pipe clamp comprises a first frame half and a second frame half joined at a hinge end by a hinge and releasably joined at a latch end by a latch. In a closed condition the two frame halves define a frame having an annular guide and three radial guides. Three jaw carriages are each slidable radially in a respective one of the radial guides, and each jaw carriage carries a jaw pad that is pivotally mounted on the jaw carriage and has a concave bearing face directed inwardly of the frame. A segmented cam ring is received in the annular guide and comprises a first cam ring segment carried by the first frame half and a second cam ring segment carried by the second frame half. The cam ring segments have complementary coupling faces at the hinge end and complementary coupling faces at the latch end, and those faces engage one another as the frame halves are closed, so that in the closed condition the two segments move in the annular guide as a coupled ring. Each jaw carriage has a follower received in a respective oblique slot of the segmented cam ring, each oblique slot being inclined to a radial direction of the frame so that rotation of the coupled ring through a stroke advances the three jaw carriages inwardly in synchronism. A wedge movable by the latch bears on a ring drive face of the segmented cam ring, so that movement of the latch rotates the coupled ring through the stroke, and a carriage return spring biases each jaw carriage outwardly so that releasing the latch retracts the jaws.

Because the coupled ring is a single kinematic element spanning both frame halves, the three jaw carriages are not driven independently. One latch motion, applied at one point on the cam ring, is distributed by the coupled ring to every jaw carriage, and the jaw pads close on the pipe together. The pivoting concave jaw pads seat on the pipe without a part dimensioned to a particular pipe diameter.

In a further aspect, the latch has a first position in which the frame halves are held around the pipe without clamping and a second, over-center position in which the wedge drives the cam ring and the pipe is clamped, and a force-limiting spring stack lies in the drive train between a latch handle and the wedge. Travel of the handle that remains after all three jaw pads have contacted the pipe compresses the force-limiting spring stack instead of advancing one jaw carriage farther than the others. An indicator pin moves relative to a fixed edge of a latch housing when the force-limiting spring stack enters a predetermined compression range, giving the operator a mechanical report of clamp loading that is produced by the state of the spring stack and not by the diameter of the pipe.

In a further aspect, the frame carries a mounting boss outside the annular guide for supporting a sensor, a tool, or a conduit, the mounting boss not participating in the advance of the three jaw carriages inwardly in synchronism, so that equipment carried on the frame is outside the path by which the jaws are synchronized.

In a further aspect, a method of clamping a pipe comprises opening the latch, swinging the frame halves apart about the hinge, placing the frame halves around a pipe, closing the frame halves so that the complementary coupling faces engage and couple the cam ring segments, moving the latch to the first position, and moving the latch to the second, over-center position so that the wedge rotates the coupled ring and the followers advance the three jaw carriages inwardly in synchronism until the jaw pads seat on the pipe. Releasing the latch unloads the force-limiting spring stack and the carriage return springs retract the jaw carriages before the frame halves are opened.
