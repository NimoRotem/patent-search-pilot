Equipment is frequently attached to the outside of a pipe after the pipe has been installed. Sensors, small tools, brackets, and lengths of conduit all have to be carried on a pipe that cannot be taken out of service, cannot be lifted, and has no free end over which a closed ring could be threaded. A clamp intended for that duty therefore has to open from the side, close around the pipe, and grip it firmly enough that the carried equipment keeps its position, while remaining removable so that the same clamp can be taken off and used again elsewhere.

A clamp that opens from the side is split into parts, and the split is what makes the gripping problem difficult. Once the clamp body is divided, any drive element that runs around the body is divided with it. If the gripping surfaces of each part are driven separately, the fixed relationship between the two parts of the drive is given up: the two sides of the clamp advance by amounts that depend on how each side is operated, so the clamp tends to settle off center on the pipe, and the load is shared unevenly between the gripping surfaces. If the split body is instead pulled closed on the pipe, the closing force passes through the joint at the split and the pipe itself reacts the closing motion, so the grip depends on how nearly the bore of the clamp matches the outside diameter of the pipe, and a part dimensioned to a particular pipe diameter is needed, so that a different pipe size calls for a different part.

Reusable clamping also has to deal with the force the operator applies. A hand lever gives the operator no measure of what has been done to the pipe. When the gripping elements have reached the pipe, further travel of the lever has to go somewhere, and in a rigid drive train it goes into whichever gripping element happens to touch first, so that one gripping surface is loaded far more than the others. Concentrated loading of a single gripping surface is undesirable, and the operator has no indication that it has occurred. An added instrument that responds to the position of the gripping elements reports the size of the pipe rather than the force in the grip.

It would be desirable to provide a split clamp in which the drive that advances the gripping elements is itself coupled by the act of closing the clamp into a single element that moves as one ring, in which every gripping element is advanced from that one drive so that the gripping elements advance together over a range of pipe sizes, and in which the travel remaining in the operator's lever after the gripping elements have landed on the pipe is absorbed within the clamp and reported to the operator as a loading condition rather than as a dimension.
