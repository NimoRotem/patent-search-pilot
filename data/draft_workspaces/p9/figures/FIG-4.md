# FIG. 4

Enlarged fragmentary view of the latch end, closed, parts in section, viewed along the frame axis from the side opposite that from which FIG. 1 is viewed. Radially outward is toward the top and the circumferential direction runs left to right. Cut solid material carries section hatching, the wedge 52, the latch housing 58, each cam ring segment and each frame body at a slant different from that of every other cut element beside it; black line work on white, well inside the drawing area. Shown schematically: the latch housing and its cavity; the attitude and outline of the latch handle 50; and the drawn path of the force-limiting spring stack 54, shown as one spring symbol.

At the lower left a hatched body holds a hatched arcuate band; at the lower right a second such body holds a like band. The left band is the second cam ring segment 24 and the right band is the first cam ring segment 22. The two bodies meet at a parting line, where the band ends face one another along complementary surfaces.

One band presents the ring drive face 36 at the place where the wedge 52 meets it. The band that carries it, its position along that band, and the form, extent and contact geometry of the face are not disclosed and are to carry no technical assertion; draw only a bare generic face at the point of contact.

Above the parting line a hatched enclosure, the latch housing 58, reaches across both bodies, its walls enclosing a cavity that is open toward the band carrying the ring drive face 36. A block, the wedge 52, lies in that cavity and bears on the ring drive face 36, its advance moving the coupled ring circumferentially in the sense that advances the jaw carriages inwardly, the direction of that movement in this view being unspecified.

In the cavity a zigzag line runs upward from the wedge 52 to the lower end of the latch handle 50. That line is the force-limiting spring stack 54, lying in the drive train between the latch handle 50 and the wedge 52.

The latch handle 50 stands above the latch housing 58 as a plain straight bar of even width, rising up and to the right from the top wall, its attitude shown schematically and drawn only to represent the latch in the over-center position, asserting no particular angle or over-center geometry. Its lower end passes through an opening in the top wall of the latch housing 58 and meets the upper end of the force-limiting spring stack 54, so the bar is continuous with the drive train; the form of that meeting is shown schematically and asserts no pivot or mounting detail.

A slender pin, the indicator pin 56, rises from the force-limiting spring stack 54 and projects beyond the outer surface of the top wall, that surface being the fixed edge against which the pin is read.
