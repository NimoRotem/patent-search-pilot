# FIG. 5

Enlarged view of the condensate channel region of FIG. 1 enclosed by the broken-line curve there designated FIG. 5, seen horizontally in section on the right side of the carrier. Plain black line work on white, diagonal section hatching on cut material. All line work lies well inside the drawing area, clear of the margins.

A thick hatched upright insulating wall body occupies the right portion of the drawing area, running from the upper part of the fragment down to a thick hatched horizontal bottom body that occupies the lower part and extends leftward.

In the upper left part of the drawing area, a hatched portion of the sectioned spacer frame is drawn in section. An open vertical channel passes down through this sectioned spacer frame, with hatched material on each side of it apart from the openings described below. This channel is one of the guide ducts 50. At the lower end of the channel, an opening passes through the downwardly facing bottom surface of the sectioned spacer frame. This opening is one of the discharge openings 76. Where that channel meets the open vertical space below, a further opening passes through the outboard face of the sectioned spacer frame, so that the channel also opens sideways into that space, as in FIG. 4.

Below the sectioned spacer frame, a thin hatched upright wall is drawn in section, spaced to the left of the inner surface of the upright wall body, and a thin hatched horizontal bottom wall joined to the lower end of that upright wall and extending leftward, the space beneath that bottom wall being open. A series of small openings passes through the product tray where that upright wall meets that bottom wall, as in FIG. 1 and FIG. 4. An open vertical space runs down between the upright wall body and the thin wall, so that the downward opening in the sectioned spacer frame stands above that space.

At the bottom of the space outboard of the thin upright wall, below the discharge opening 76, a shallow upwardly open trough is drawn in section, shown schematically. This trough is the condensate channel 70.

An elongated body of absorbent material lies in the trough, shown schematically. This body is the absorbent wick 72. A narrow strip, shown schematically, extends from one end of the absorbent body, its free end clear of the absorbent body. This strip is the pull tab 74.

## Numerals shown on this figure

- 50: inside the open vertical channel in the sectioned spacer frame.
- 70: inside the trough, clear of the absorbent body.
- 72: well inside the elongated absorbent body lying in the trough.
- 74: well inside the narrow strip extending from the absorbent body.
- 76: on the opening through the bottom surface of the sectioned spacer frame.
