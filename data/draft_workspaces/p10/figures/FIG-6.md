# FIG. 6

Fragmentary elevation of the outside of the outer shell at the mechanical exposure indicator, seen looking horizontally at the outer surface of one side wall, with the indicator stem in the exposed position. Plain black line work on white, no hatching. All line work lies well inside the drawing area, clear of the margins.

A plain rectangle in the central portion of the drawing area is a fragment of the outer surface of one of the side walls 14 of the outer shell 10. All four edges of the rectangle are drawn as break lines, showing that the wall continues beyond the fragment on every side.

Within that rectangle, a smaller plain rectangular outline, shown schematically, is the outward-facing end of the mechanical exposure indicator 80.

Within that smaller outline, a small plain square, shown schematically, is the outer end face of the indicator stem 82, seen end on where the stem projects outward from the wall body in the exposed position.

A vertical broken line crosses the fragment through the smaller outline, beginning a short distance above the upper edge of the fragment and ending a short distance below its lower edge, both ends remaining well inside the drawing area. This broken line is the cutting-plane line for the sectional view FIG. 7. Its upper end carries a short arrow pointing toward the left side of the drawing area and, beside that arrow, the section designation 7. Its lower end carries a second short arrow, also pointing toward the left side of the drawing area, and beside that arrow the section designation 7 is repeated. Both arrows therefore point in the same direction, which is the direction in which FIG. 7 is seen, and the repeated section designation 7 at the two ends makes the cutting-plane line read line 7-7.

## Numerals shown on this figure

- 10: outside the fragment, with a lead line and arrow to the outer shell as a whole.
- 14: well inside the rectangle, clear of the smaller outline and of the vertical broken line.
- 80: on the smaller rectangular outline, clear of the small square.
- 82: well inside the small square.
