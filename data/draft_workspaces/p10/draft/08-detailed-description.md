In the following description, directional terms such as upper, lower, above, below, inward and outward refer to the carrier in its normal upright orientation with the insulated lid uppermost. The same reference numeral denotes the same part throughout the drawings.

Overall arrangement

Referring to FIG. 1, a passive cold-chain carrier 100 includes an insulated outer shell 10, a product tray 20 removably received in the outer shell 10, a thermal module 40 removably received in the outer shell 10 above the product tray 20, and an insulated lid 60 closable on an upper edge of the outer shell 10. The outer shell 10 has a floor 12 and side walls 14 that rise from the floor 12 and bound an interior of the outer shell 10. The outer shell 10 and the insulated lid 60 are formed of thermally insulating material. The carrier 100 is reusable, the product tray 20 and the thermal module 40 being removable from the outer shell 10.

The product tray 20 is an upwardly open receptacle having a bottom wall and tray side walls 26, and receives the goods to be transported. A product region is the space within the product tray 20 that is occupied by those goods. Tray feet 22 depend from the product tray 20 and stand on the floor 12, so that the product tray 20 is supported above the floor 12 and a lower return passage 24 remains between the bottom wall of the product tray 20 and the floor 12.

The tray side walls 26 are spaced inward from the side walls 14 of the outer shell 10, so that two side supply passages 28 are formed, each side supply passage 28 extending downward between one tray side wall 26 and the confronting side wall 14. Perforations 30 through the product tray 20 open from the side supply passages 28 into the lower return passage 24. One tray side wall 26, one side supply passage 28 and the perforations 30 are seen in FIG. 4.

The thermal module

Referring to FIG. 2, the thermal module 40 includes a rigid spacer frame 42 and two opposed phase-change cassettes held by the spacer frame 42, namely a first phase-change cassette 44 and a second phase-change cassette 46. Each phase-change cassette holds a phase-change material, and the two cassettes are held by the spacer frame 42 in a mutually facing arrangement so that an inner face of the first phase-change cassette 44 confronts an inner face of the second phase-change cassette 46 across a central space of the spacer frame 42. The phase-change cassettes are removable from the spacer frame 42, so that conditioned cassettes are fitted to the spacer frame 42 before packing.

The spacer frame 42 rests above the product tray 20 and below the insulated lid 60. The spacer frame 42 defines a central inlet opening 48 that opens downward into the central space between the two phase-change cassettes, so that air rising from the product region enters the central space between the confronting inner faces. The spacer frame 42 further defines two downward guide ducts 50, one of which is seen in FIG. 4, each guide duct 50 located beside one of the phase-change cassettes, and peripheral outlet openings 54 positioned to align with the side supply passages 28.

The spacer frame 42 also defines a mating channel 56 that opens upward at a top region of the spacer frame 42, and carries resilient feet 58. The mating channel 56 and the depending indexing rib 62 described below, which enters it from above, are shaped so that engagement between them during the closing travel translates the spacer frame 42 horizontally into position, rather than merely resting one upon the other.

Registration and retention by the lid

The insulated lid 60 carries a depending indexing rib 62 on an underside of the insulated lid 60, and a compressible lid gasket 64 carried on the underside of the insulated lid 60 and positioned to bear against the upper edge of the outer shell 10. The depending indexing rib 62, the mating channel 56 of the spacer frame 42 and the compressible lid gasket 64 are seen in FIG. 3. As the insulated lid 60 is closed, the depending indexing rib 62 enters the mating channel 56 of the spacer frame 42 and shifts the thermal module 40 into a registered position in which the peripheral outlet openings 54 overlap the side supply passages 28. The engagement is progressive: the depending indexing rib 62 first meets the mating channel 56 while the lid is still spaced above the outer shell 10, and the remaining closing travel translates the spacer frame 42 horizontally into the registered position.

This sequence addresses the practical problem that the circulation path depends on an alignment which a person packing quickly at a bench cannot be relied upon to produce. The thermal module 40 is placed in the outer shell 10 above the product tray 20, and the registration is completed by the closing of the lid. The alignment on which the circulation path depends is therefore created by an act the packer cannot omit, because a carrier that is not closed does not ship. The compressible lid gasket 64 bears against the upper edge of the outer shell 10 and is compressed only at the end of the closing travel, after the depending indexing rib 62 has completed registration, so that the carrier cannot be sealed shut around a thermal module 40 that is still out of position.

The same closing movement retains the thermal module 40. Ledges 18 of the outer shell 10 lie beneath the resilient feet 58 of the spacer frame 42, one such ledge 18 and one such resilient foot 58 being seen in FIG. 3, and closure of the insulated lid 60 presses the resilient feet 58 against the ledges 18. Closure of the insulated lid 60 compresses the resilient feet 58 between the spacer frame 42 and the ledges 18, so that the thermal module 40 is held down and cannot lift away from the product tray 20 during transport.

The circulation path

In the registered position, the outer shell 10, the product tray 20, the thermal module 40 and the insulated lid 60 cooperate to define a circulation path. Air warmed by the goods rises from the product region within the product tray 20 and enters the central inlet opening 48. The rising stream divides along the faces of the first phase-change cassette 44 and the second phase-change cassette 46, giving two cooling streams, and each cooled stream descends through one of the guide ducts 50 beside the phase-change cassettes, leaves the thermal module 40 through the peripheral outlet openings 54, and enters the side supply passage 28 registered beneath that peripheral outlet opening 54. Each descending stream then passes through the perforations 30 in the product tray 20 and returns through the lower return passage 24 beneath the product tray 20.

The carrier 100 is free of a fan, a pump and any powered air mover. Air warmed in the product region rises to the central inlet opening 48, and air cooled along the faces of the phase-change cassettes descends through the guide ducts 50 and the side supply passages 28, so that circulation proceeds without any powered air mover.

Condensate management

A condensate channel 70 is a shallow upwardly open trough extending around the product tray 20. A removable absorbent wick 72 lies in the condensate channel 70, and the absorbent wick 72 remains in the outer shell 10 when the product tray 20 is removed. Discharge openings 76 are provided at lower ends of the guide ducts 50 and discharge condensate into the condensate channel 70. The condensate channel 70, the absorbent wick 72, the pull tab 74 described below, one discharge opening 76 and the lower end of one guide duct 50 are seen in FIG. 5. Condensate present in the guide ducts 50 runs down them and is delivered through the discharge openings 76 into the condensate channel 70 rather than onto the goods.

The absorbent wick 72 carries a pull tab 74 that remains accessible when the product tray 20 is removed from the outer shell 10. A saturated wick is therefore drawn out and replaced as a service item between shipments, by lifting out the product tray 20 and pulling the pull tab 74, without opening a phase-change cassette and without taking the cassettes out of service to dry the interior.

Mechanical exposure indicator

The outer shell 10 carries a mechanical exposure indicator 80 visible from outside the carrier 100. FIG. 6 shows the mechanical exposure indicator 80 as it appears from outside the outer shell 10. Referring to FIG. 7, the mechanical exposure indicator 80 includes an indicator stem 82 movable between a stowed position and an exposed position, a temperature-responsive latch 84 that retains the indicator stem 82 in the stowed position, and a spring 86 biasing the indicator stem 82 toward the exposed position. The indicator stem 82 is thermally coupled to the first phase-change cassette 44. FIG. 6 and FIG. 7 show the indicator stem 82 in the exposed position.

The temperature-responsive latch 84 is arranged to release when the first phase-change cassette 44 softens. On release, the spring 86 moves the indicator stem 82 to the exposed position, in which the indicator stem 82 is visible from outside the outer shell 10.

The exposed position is a latched position. The mechanical exposure indicator 80 retains the indicator stem 82 in the exposed position after the first phase-change cassette 44 has cooled again, and the mechanical exposure indicator 80 is reset manually only after the carrier 100 is opened. A receiving inspection therefore sees the excursion even though the carrier presents itself as cold and the cassette as firm, and the record cannot be erased in transit by the cassette recovering. The indication is produced by the state of the refrigerant on which the payload depends rather than by a sensor at an arbitrary point in the air space, and requires no battery, no reading device and no data transfer.

The mechanical exposure indicator 80 does not control airflow. It neither opens, closes nor throttles the central inlet opening 48, the guide ducts 50, the peripheral outlet openings 54, the side supply passages 28, the perforations 30 or the lower return passage 24, and the circulation path is the same whether or not the mechanical exposure indicator 80 has moved to the exposed position. The behaviour of the carrier 100 is therefore unchanged by the act of reporting, and a tripped indicator does not degrade the protection given to the payload for the remainder of the journey.

Operation

In use, conditioned phase-change cassettes are fitted to the spacer frame 42 as the first phase-change cassette 44 and the second phase-change cassette 46. Goods are placed in the product tray 20 and the product tray 20 is placed in the outer shell 10 so that the tray feet 22 stand on the floor 12, leaving the lower return passage 24 beneath the product tray 20 and the two side supply passages 28 between the tray side walls 26 and the side walls 14. The thermal module 40 is placed in the outer shell 10 above the product tray 20. The insulated lid 60 is then closed, whereby the depending indexing rib 62 enters the mating channel 56 and shifts the thermal module 40 into the registered position, the resilient feet 58 are pressed against the ledges 18 to retain the thermal module 40, and the compressible lid gasket 64 seals against the upper edge of the outer shell 10. Air thereafter circulates along the circulation path without operation of any powered air mover. At the destination, the insulated lid 60 is opened, the mechanical exposure indicator 80 is read and, if it stands in the exposed position, reset manually, the product tray 20 is lifted out with the goods, and the absorbent wick 72 is drawn out by the pull tab 74 and replaced if saturated.

Essential and optional features

Two phase-change cassettes are held by the spacer frame 42, opposed across the central space. Entry of the depending indexing rib 62 into the mating channel 56 during closing travel shifts the thermal module 40 into the registered position. The peripheral outlet openings 54 overlap the side supply passages 28 in the registered position. The condensate channel 70, the absorbent wick 72, the pull tab 74, the discharge openings 76 and the mechanical exposure indicator 80 are optional features, and a carrier 100 may be built with the registered circulation path and the lid-driven registration alone, or with any combination of the optional features.

The described implementations are examples. The optional features identified above may be provided in any combination or omitted, and the described implementations may otherwise be varied within the scope of the claims that follow.
