## Overview of the carrier

Referring to FIG. 1, FIG. 2, and FIG. 6, a reusable passive cold-chain carrier includes an insulated outer shell 10, a removable product tray 20, a removable thermal module 40, and an insulated lid 60. The insulated outer shell 10 has a shell floor 12, shell side walls 14 rising from the shell floor 12, and an upper edge 16 at the top of the shell side walls 14. The insulated outer shell 10 and the insulated lid 60 are of insulating construction, and the carrier can be returned for reuse.

Referring further to FIG. 2, FIG. 5, and FIG. 6, the removable product tray 20 receives the payload. The removable product tray 20 has tray side walls 24 and tray feet 22. The tray feet 22 stand on the shell floor 12 and suspend the removable product tray 20 above the shell floor 12, so that a lower return passage 28 remains under the removable product tray 20. The tray side walls 24 are spaced inward from the shell side walls 14 to form two side supply passages 30. The removable product tray 20 has perforations 26 through the removable product tray 20.

The removable product tray 20 lifts out of the insulated outer shell 10 as a unit once the removable thermal module 40 has been taken out. The side supply passages 30 and the lower return passage 28 are formed by the position that the removable product tray 20 takes up when it is returned to the insulated outer shell 10.

## The removable thermal module

Referring to FIG. 1, FIG. 3, FIG. 4, and FIG. 5, the removable thermal module 40 includes a rigid spacer frame 42 holding two opposed phase-change cassettes 44. The rigid spacer frame 42 is a rigid frame body. The rigid spacer frame 42 rests above the removable product tray 20 and below the insulated lid 60, so that the removable thermal module 40 is lifted out as one piece and the two phase-change cassettes 44 travel with it.

The rigid spacer frame 42 includes a central inlet opening 46 at the centre of the rigid spacer frame 42 and facing the product region within the removable product tray 20, two downward guide ducts 48 beside the phase-change cassettes 44, and peripheral outlet openings 50. Each phase-change cassette 44 is a cassette of phase-change material that is conditioned before use, and the two phase-change cassettes 44 are held on opposite sides of the central inlet opening 46 with their faces mutually opposed. Each downward guide duct 48 extends downward beside one of the phase-change cassettes 44 and opens at its lower end. The peripheral outlet openings 50 lie at the periphery of the rigid spacer frame 42 and are aligned with the side supply passages 30 when the removable thermal module 40 is in a registered position.

Because the two phase-change cassettes 44 are opposed, the air that divides along their faces is exposed to a face of each phase-change cassette 44.

## The registered passive circulation path

The carrier is passive. It has no fan, pump, or powered air mover. Circulation is produced by warm air rising from the product region, and the geometry of the carrier gives that rising air a single closed path to follow.

In use, warm air rising from a product region within the removable product tray 20 enters the central inlet opening 46, divides along faces of the phase-change cassettes 44, descends through the downward guide ducts 48, enters the side supply passages 30, passes through the perforations 26 in the removable product tray 20, and returns through the lower return passage 28 to the product region. The path is closed on itself and surrounds the removable product tray 20 on the sides and underneath, so that cooled air reaches the payload around and beneath the removable product tray 20.

The path exists only when the peripheral outlet openings 50 overlap the side supply passages 30. The registration described below establishes that overlap.

## Lid-driven registration and retention

Referring to FIG. 1, FIG. 2, FIG. 3, and FIG. 5, the insulated lid 60 carries a depending indexing rib 62, and the rigid spacer frame 42 has a mating channel 52 that receives the depending indexing rib 62. As the insulated lid 60 closes, the depending indexing rib 62 enters the mating channel 52 and shifts the removable thermal module 40 into a registered position in which the peripheral outlet openings 50 overlap the side supply passages 30. The registration is therefore performed by the act of closing the carrier. The person who packs the carrier is not required to align the removable thermal module 40, and cannot leave it misaligned, because the insulated lid 60 does not come down onto the upper edge 16 without carrying the removable thermal module 40 into position.

Referring also to FIG. 4, a compressible lid gasket 64 carried by the insulated lid 60 seals against the upper edge 16 of the insulated outer shell 10 after registration. The registration of the removable thermal module 40 thus occurs as the insulated lid 60 is closed, and the interior is sealed by the same closing motion.

Referring to FIG. 4, the same closing movement presses resilient feet 54 of the rigid spacer frame 42 against ledges 18 of the insulated outer shell 10, so that the removable thermal module 40 cannot lift away from the removable product tray 20 during transport.

Registration and retention are thus produced by the same movement that a user has to make in any event. Nothing has to be latched, clamped, or checked separately, and there is no state in which the carrier is closed and sealed but the circulation path is not established.

## Condensate handling and the removable wick

Referring to FIG. 5 and FIG. 6, a shallow condensate channel 70 extends around the removable product tray 20, and a removable absorbent wick 72 lies in the shallow condensate channel 70. Condensate discharge openings 56 at lower ends of the downward guide ducts 48 discharge condensate into the shallow condensate channel 70. Condensate that collects within the downward guide ducts 48 runs down the ducts and leaves them through the condensate discharge openings 56 into the shallow condensate channel 70, rather than standing in the bottom of the interior.

The removable absorbent wick 72 has a pull tab 74. The pull tab 74 remains accessible when the removable product tray 20 is removed, so that the removable absorbent wick 72 is drawn out of the shallow condensate channel 70 and replaced without opening a phase-change cassette 44. Routine servicing between journeys therefore does not require handling of the phase-change cassettes 44, which are conditioned items and are not to be opened. Withdrawing the removable absorbent wick 72 from the shallow condensate channel 70 by the pull tab 74 and placing a replacement absorbent wick in the shallow condensate channel 70 restores the carrier for the next journey.

## Mechanical exposure indicator

Referring to FIG. 7, the insulated outer shell 10 includes a mechanical exposure indicator 80 visible from outside. An indicator stem 82 is thermally coupled to one phase-change cassette 44 and is retained by a temperature-responsive latch 84. A spring 86 acts on the indicator stem 82 in the outward direction, and the temperature-responsive latch 84 retains the indicator stem 82 against that action while the phase-change cassette 44 remains in condition.

If that phase-change cassette 44 softens enough to release the temperature-responsive latch 84, the spring 86 moves the indicator stem 82 to a latched exposed position. The indication remains exposed after the phase-change cassette 44 cools again, and is reset manually only after the carrier is opened. The value of this behaviour is that a payload which was warmed part way through a journey and then cooled again arrives at a temperature that looks correct, and the contents alone give the recipient no way to know what happened. The mechanical exposure indicator 80 records the event in the structure of the carrier and holds the record until a person opens the carrier and deliberately resets it.

The mechanical exposure indicator 80 does not control airflow. The circulation path is the same whether the indicator stem 82 is retained or has moved to the latched exposed position. The thermal condition being reported is taken directly from a phase-change cassette 44, that is, from the reserve that actually conditions the payload.

## Method of passively conditioning a payload

In use, conditioned phase-change cassettes 44 are fitted to the rigid spacer frame 42 to form the removable thermal module 40, products are placed in the removable product tray 20, the removable product tray 20 is placed in the insulated outer shell 10 on its tray feet 22, the removable thermal module 40 is placed above the removable product tray 20, and the insulated lid 60 is closed. Closing the insulated lid 60 brings the depending indexing rib 62 into the mating channel 52, shifts the removable thermal module 40 into the registered position in which the peripheral outlet openings 50 overlap the side supply passages 30, presses the resilient feet 54 against the ledges 18, and compresses the compressible lid gasket 64 against the upper edge 16 after that registration. Air then circulates passively along the path described above for the duration of the journey.

At the destination the insulated lid 60 is opened, which withdraws the depending indexing rib 62 from the mating channel 52, and the removable thermal module 40 is lifted out and the removable product tray 20 is lifted out. Between journeys the removable absorbent wick 72 is withdrawn from the shallow condensate channel 70 by the pull tab 74 and replaced, and the mechanical exposure indicator 80 is reset by hand if it has moved to the latched exposed position.
