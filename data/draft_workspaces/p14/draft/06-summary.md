A reusable passive cold-chain carrier is disclosed. The carrier includes an insulated outer shell, a removable product tray, a removable thermal module, and an insulated lid.

The removable product tray is suspended above a shell floor by tray feet so that a lower return passage remains under the removable product tray. Tray side walls of the removable product tray are spaced inward from shell side walls of the insulated outer shell to form two side supply passages. The removable product tray has perforations.

The removable thermal module includes a rigid spacer frame holding two opposed phase-change cassettes. The rigid spacer frame rests above the removable product tray and below the insulated lid. The rigid spacer frame includes a central inlet opening, two downward guide ducts beside the phase-change cassettes, and peripheral outlet openings that are aligned with the side supply passages when the removable thermal module is in a registered position.

The carrier is passive and has no fan, pump, or powered air mover. Warm air rising from a product region enters the central inlet opening, divides along faces of the phase-change cassettes, descends through the downward guide ducts, enters the side supply passages, passes through the perforations in the removable product tray, and returns through the lower return passage. The circulation path therefore surrounds the payload rather than acting on it from one side, and it is driven by the rise of warm air from the product region, without any powered air mover.

The insulated lid carries a depending indexing rib. As the insulated lid closes, the depending indexing rib enters a mating channel in the rigid spacer frame and shifts the removable thermal module into a registered position in which the peripheral outlet openings overlap the side supply passages. The act of closing the carrier therefore establishes the circulation path, instead of relying on the person who packs the carrier to place the removable thermal module accurately. A compressible lid gasket seals against an upper edge of the insulated outer shell after registration, so the removable thermal module is aligned before the interior is closed off. The same closing movement presses resilient feet of the rigid spacer frame against ledges of the insulated outer shell so that the removable thermal module cannot lift away from the removable product tray during transport.

In some embodiments, a removable absorbent wick lies in a shallow condensate channel around the removable product tray, and condensate discharge openings at lower ends of the downward guide ducts discharge condensate into the shallow condensate channel. A pull tab on the removable absorbent wick remains accessible when the removable product tray is removed, allowing the removable absorbent wick to be replaced without opening a phase-change cassette.

In some embodiments, the insulated outer shell includes a mechanical exposure indicator visible from outside. An indicator stem is thermally coupled to one phase-change cassette and retained by a temperature-responsive latch. If that phase-change cassette softens enough to release the temperature-responsive latch, a spring moves the indicator stem to a latched exposed position. The indication remains exposed after the phase-change cassette cools again and is reset manually only after the carrier is opened. The mechanical exposure indicator does not control airflow.

Methods are also disclosed, including a method of passively conditioning a payload in which conditioned phase-change cassettes are fitted to the rigid spacer frame, products are placed in the removable product tray, the removable thermal module is placed above the removable product tray, and the insulated lid is closed to register and retain the removable thermal module, and a method of servicing the carrier between uses.
