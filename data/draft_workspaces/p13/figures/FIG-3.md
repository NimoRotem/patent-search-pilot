# FIG. 3

A schematic diagram of the synchronizing transmission and the worm drive of the platform, the two transverse shafts, the sprockets, the chain loop, the worm wheel, and the worm input shaft being laid out diagrammatically in one plane rather than in a single projection. All line work stays well inside the drawing area, clear of the filing margins.


A front transverse shaft 22 is a straight horizontal line of uniform thickness across the lower part of the drawing area, a short irregular break line closing each of its two ends.

A rear transverse shaft 24 is a straight horizontal line of the same thickness across the upper part of the drawing area, a short irregular break line closing each of its two ends.

A front sprocket 26 is a toothed circle centred on the front transverse shaft 22 between its broken ends, fixed to that shaft, shown schematically.

A rear sprocket 28 is a toothed circle centred on the rear transverse shaft 24 between its broken ends, fixed to that shaft and lying in the plane of the chain loop with the front sprocket 26, shown schematically.

An endless synchronizing chain 30 is drawn as one endless closed loop passing around the front sprocket 26 and the rear sprocket 28 and coupling them, shown schematically.

A worm wheel 34 is a toothed circle centred on the front transverse shaft 22 between its broken ends, fixed to that shaft and spaced along the shaft from the front sprocket 26, shown schematically.

A worm input shaft 32 is a straight vertical line running from the worm wheel 34 toward the lower part of the drawing area, a short irregular break line closing its lower end. A short barrel shape on it, at the end nearer the worm wheel 34, meets the toothed edge of the worm wheel 34 and represents the worm, shown schematically.
