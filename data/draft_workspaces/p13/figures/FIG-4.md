# FIG. 4

An enlarged fragmentary vertical sectional view through one guide tower and the corner post sliding in it, taken on line 4-4 of FIG. 2, on a vertical plane through the locking pawl of that guide tower, the locking pawl shown in its locked position. Sectioned solids carry hatching, the locking pawl 40 and the locked-state flag 54 being shown in elevation rather than sectioned. A short irregular break line closes each end of the fragment, both well inside the drawing area.


A guide tower 14 appears in section as two upright hatched wall bands, a left and a right wall band, running between the break lines and spaced apart across the drawing area, shown schematically.

A corner post 16 appears in section as an upright hatched column between the two wall bands, spaced from each of them, the space on the rack side accommodating the locking pawl 40, shown schematically.

A rack 18 is a row of teeth of constant pitch along the side of the corner post 16 that faces the right wall band, the row running the full sectioned length of that column, the teeth shown schematically.

A locking pawl 40 is a solid body shown in elevation and therefore without hatching, in the space between the corner post 16 and the right wall band, at mid height of the fragment, its left end engaging the rack 18, shown schematically.

A pawl pivot 42 is a small circle with a centre mark in the body of the locking pawl 40, mounted to the guide tower 14, shown schematically.

A compression spring 44 is a coiled line acting between the guide tower 14 and the body of the locking pawl 40 so as to bias the locking pawl 40 toward the locked position, shown schematically.

A locked-state flag 54 is a small tab carried by the body of the locking pawl 40 and aligned with the window 56, shown schematically.

A window 56 is a rectangular opening through the right wall band, drawn as an unhatched break in that band bounded by a short horizontal line above and below, the locked-state flag 54 visible in the opening.
