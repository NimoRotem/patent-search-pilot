# Invention disclosure (the inventor's own words)

This is the ONLY authority for what the invention includes. Everything you claim must be supported here or in later messages from the user.

The invention is a reusable hand tool that locates and guides two drill holes for a cabinet pull without requiring a powered measuring system. The tool has an elongated straight rail with a flat lower face that rests on the front face of a cabinet door or drawer front. A straight longitudinal slot runs through the rail. Distance marks are printed along the upper face beside the slot.

A fixed fence is attached at the first end of the rail. The fence extends downward from the lower face and bears against one straight edge of the workpiece. The fence is perpendicular to the rail, so the rail extends inward across the workpiece when the fence bears against the edge. A removable spacer plate can be fitted between the fence and the workpiece edge. The spacer plate has parallel opposite faces and changes the setback of every guided hole by its thickness without changing the separation between the holes.

Two separate guide carriages sit on the upper face of the rail. Each carriage has a lower key that passes into the longitudinal slot, and the key prevents the carriage from rotating while allowing it to slide along the slot. Each carriage carries one cylindrical drill bushing with a vertical bore. The bore passes completely through the carriage and aligns with an opening through the slot so a drill bit can reach the workpiece. The two bushing axes are parallel to one another and perpendicular to the flat lower face of the rail.

Each carriage has a threaded clamp knob above the rail. A threaded shank of the knob passes through the carriage and the longitudinal slot into a clamping shoe below the rail. Turning the knob draws the shoe toward the carriage and clamps the rail between the carriage and the shoe. Loosening the knob allows the carriage to slide. The first carriage has a pointer aligned with its bushing axis and readable against the distance marks. The second carriage has its own pointer aligned with its bushing axis. The difference between the two pointer readings is therefore the center-to-center hole spacing.

The rail has a transverse center index at its midpoint. A shallow V-shaped sight notch is formed in the second end of the rail and aligned with the center index. The user can align the center index and the sight notch with a pencil centerline on the workpiece before tightening both carriage knobs. The fence controls setback from the workpiece edge, while the two pointer readings control hole separation and the position of the pair relative to the centerline.

The fence is reversible. It is secured to the first end of the rail by two screws received in two corresponding holes. Removing the screws, turning the fence over, and reinstalling the screws moves the downwardly projecting part of the fence from one longitudinal side of the rail to the opposite longitudinal side. This lets the same marked upper face be used when the tool is referenced from either an upper edge or a lower edge of a workpiece.

To use the tool, the user selects the desired hole spacing, loosens both clamp knobs, slides the carriages until their pointer readings have the desired difference and are centered about the transverse center index, and tightens the knobs. The user places the lower face on the workpiece, presses the fence or the installed spacer plate against the selected workpiece edge, aligns the sight notch and center index with the pencil centerline, and drills through both bushings. The tool is then lifted away. No part remains attached to the workpiece.

The rail, fence, carriage bodies, shoes, spacer plate, pointers, and knobs may be made from rigid polymer or metal. The drill bushings are replaceable wear parts and are made from hardened metal. The tool has no motor, battery, electronic sensor, or network connection.
