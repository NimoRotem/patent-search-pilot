In the following description, the terms upper, lower, above, below, inward, and downward refer to the orientation of the drilling jig when its flat lower face rests on the front face of a workpiece. These terms are used for convenience of description and do not limit the orientation in which the jig may be used.

Referring to FIG. 1, the drilling jig includes an elongated straight rail 10. The rail 10 has a flat lower face 14 (FIG. 4) that rests on the front face of a workpiece 90, and an opposite upper face 12 (FIG. 5). A straight longitudinal slot 16 (FIG. 2) runs through the rail 10 from the upper face 12 to the lower face 14 and extends along the length of the rail 10. Distance marks 18 (FIG. 2) are carried on the upper face 12 beside the longitudinal slot 16 and are printed there. The rail 10 has a first end and an opposite second end.

The workpiece 90 is a cabinet door or a drawer front having a front face and at least one straight workpiece edge 92. The jig locates and guides the two holes for a cabinet pull, and it does so in a single placement of the jig on the front face of the workpiece 90.

A fence 30 is secured to the first end of the rail 10. The fence 30 is a fixed fence, by which is meant that it occupies a fixed position at the first end of the rail 10 and is not slidable along the rail 10 as the guide carriages described below are. No setting of the jig is made by moving the fence 30 along the rail 10, and no position along the length of the rail 10 other than the first end is available to it. A fixed fence in this sense is not a permanent one. As described below, the fence 30 is releasably secured to the rail 10 and may be taken off and reinstalled in either of two orientations, and it occupies the same fixed position at the first end of the rail 10 in both of those orientations. The fence 30 includes a downwardly projecting portion 32 that stands on one longitudinal side of the rail 10 and extends below the lower face 14 of the rail 10. As seen in FIG. 1, the downwardly projecting portion 32 bears against the workpiece edge 92. The fence 30 is perpendicular to the rail 10, so that when the downwardly projecting portion 32 is held against the workpiece edge 92 the rail 10 extends inward across the front face of the workpiece 90. The fence 30 therefore fixes the setback of the jig, and of every hole guided by the jig, from the workpiece edge 92.

Referring to FIG. 6, the fence 30 is releasably secured to the first end of the rail 10 by two screws 34 received in two corresponding holes 36 in the fence. The fence 30 is reversible. In a first orientation, the downwardly projecting portion 32 lies on a first longitudinal side of the rail 10. The fence 30 is securable in a second orientation upon removal of the two screws 34, inversion of the fence 30, and reinstallation of the two screws 34 in the same two holes 36, and in that second orientation the downwardly projecting portion 32 lies on the opposite second longitudinal side of the rail 10. In both orientations the upper face 12, and the distance marks 18 carried on it, face away from the workpiece 90 and remain visible to the user, and the distance marks 18 read in the same direction along the rail 10.

The reversible mounting of the fence 30 addresses a practical difficulty of edge-referenced hole location. Reversing the fence 30 alone moves the downwardly projecting portion 32 to the other longitudinal side of the rail 10 while leaving the rail 10, the distance marks 18, and the carriage settings undisturbed.

A removable spacer plate 40, shown in FIG. 4, may be fitted between the fence 30 and the workpiece edge 92. The spacer plate 40 has parallel opposite faces, one of which bears against the downwardly projecting portion 32 and the other of which bears against the workpiece edge 92. Because the two faces of the spacer plate 40 are parallel, the spacer plate 40 increases the setback of every hole guided by the jig by the thickness of the spacer plate 40 and does not change the separation between the holes. A second setback may therefore be obtained by fitting the spacer plate 40 without disturbing a hole spacing that has already been set. The spacer plate 40 is fitted between the fence 30 and the workpiece edge 92 and is removable.

Referring again to FIG. 1, a first guide carriage 50 and a second guide carriage 70 are seated separately on the rail 10, one nearer to the fence 30 than the other. The two guide carriages are separate bodies that are uncoupled from one another, and each is positioned along the rail 10 and clamped to the rail 10 independently of the other.

The construction of the guide carriages is shown in FIG. 5, a cross-sectional view taken on line 5-5 of FIG. 2 through the first guide carriage 50. The second guide carriage 70 is of the same construction as the first guide carriage 50. Each guide carriage has a body that seats on the upper face 12 of the rail 10 and a key that depends from the body and passes into the longitudinal slot 16. The first guide carriage 50 has a key 52 (FIG. 5) and the second guide carriage 70 has a like key 72. The key 52 prevents rotation of the first guide carriage 50 relative to the rail 10 while permitting the first guide carriage 50 to slide along the longitudinal slot 16, and the key 72 of the second guide carriage 70 does the same for the second guide carriage 70.

Each guide carriage carries one cylindrical drill bushing, a drill bushing 54 (FIG. 5) in the first guide carriage 50 and a drill bushing 74 (FIG. 3) in the second guide carriage 70. Each drill bushing has a bore that passes completely through the guide carriage that carries it and registers with the longitudinal slot 16, so that a drill bit passed down that bore travels through the longitudinal slot 16 and reaches the front face of the workpiece 90 beneath the rail 10. The bore of the drill bushing 54 of the first guide carriage 50 and the bore of the drill bushing 74 of the second guide carriage 70 are parallel to one another and perpendicular to the flat lower face 14 of the rail 10, and the key 52 and the key 72 of the second guide carriage 70 hold their guide carriages against rotation relative to the rail 10 during the drilling, so that each hole is started and driven square to the front face of the workpiece 90 at the position set on the rail 10.

The drill bushing 54 and the drill bushing 74 are replaceable wear parts made of hardened metal.

Each guide carriage has a threaded clamp knob above the rail 10, a clamp knob 58 (FIGS. 1 and 5) on the first guide carriage 50 and a clamp knob 78 (FIG. 2) on the second guide carriage 70. A threaded shank of the clamp knob passes downward through the body of the guide carriage and through the longitudinal slot 16 and is received in a clamping shoe below the rail 10, a clamping shoe 60 (FIG. 5) in the case of the first guide carriage 50 and a like clamping shoe 80 in the case of the second guide carriage 70. Turning the clamp knob in the tightening direction draws the clamping shoe upward toward the guide carriage, so that the rail 10 is clamped between the body of the guide carriage bearing on the upper face 12 and the clamping shoe bearing on the lower face 14. The guide carriage is thereby locked at a chosen position along the rail 10. Turning the clamp knob in the loosening direction releases the clamping shoe from the lower face 14 and permits the guide carriage to slide freely along the longitudinal slot 16. The clamping shoe of each guide carriage is disposed below the lower face 14 of the rail 10 and bears against the lower face 14 when the clamp knob of that guide carriage is tightened, and is released from the lower face 14 when that clamp knob is loosened.

The first guide carriage 50 carries a first pointer 62 and the second guide carriage 70 carries a second pointer 82, as seen in FIG. 3. The first pointer 62 is aligned with the axis of the bore of the drill bushing 54 of the first guide carriage 50 and is readable against the distance marks 18. The second pointer 82 is aligned with the axis of the bore of the drill bushing 74 of the second guide carriage 70 and is readable against the distance marks 18. Each pointer is an index feature that stands adjacent the distance marks 18 on the upper face 12.

Because each guide carriage carries its own pointer and each pointer is read against the same distance marks 18, the position of each hole of the pair is established by its own direct reading. The difference between the reading of the first pointer 62 and the reading of the second pointer 82 is the center-to-center spacing of the two holes that the jig will guide. The position of each guide carriage along the rail 10 is therefore set by its own reading against the distance marks 18, independently of the position of the other guide carriage.

Referring to FIG. 7, the rail 10 has a transverse center index 24 at its midpoint. The transverse center index 24 is a mark that is carried on the upper face 12 and runs transverse to the length of the rail 10, marking the point midway between the ends of the rail 10. A shallow V-shaped sight notch 26 (FIG. 7) is formed in the second end of the rail 10 and is aligned with the transverse center index 24, for alignment with a pencil centerline 94 (FIG. 7) drawn on the workpiece 90. The transverse center index 24 and the sight notch 26 are aligned with one another, and the user aligns the transverse center index 24 and the sight notch 26 together with the centerline 94 before drilling.

The settings of the jig are made as follows. The fence 30, with or without the spacer plate 40, sets the setback of both holes from the workpiece edge 92. The readings of the first pointer 62 and the second pointer 82 against the distance marks 18 set the separation between the two holes and the position of the pair relative to the centerline 94. The transverse center index 24 and the sight notch 26 are aligned with the centerline 94.

A method of locating and guiding the pair of holes with the jig is carried out by providing the jig and then proceeding as follows. The user first selects the center-to-center spacing of the pull to be installed. The clamp knob 58 and the clamp knob 78 are loosened. The first guide carriage 50 and the second guide carriage 70 are slid along the longitudinal slot 16 until the reading of the first pointer 62 and the reading of the second pointer 82 against the distance marks 18 differ by the selected spacing and are centered about the transverse center index 24, that is, until the two readings lie at equal distances on opposite sides of the transverse center index 24. The clamp knob 58 and the clamp knob 78 are then tightened, locking each guide carriage to the rail 10.

The user marks a centerline 94 on the front face of the workpiece 90 and places the flat lower face 14 of the rail 10 on that front face. Where an increased setback is wanted, the spacer plate 40 is fitted between the downwardly projecting portion 32 and the workpiece edge 92 before the jig is pressed against that edge. The downwardly projecting portion 32 of the fence 30, or the outer face of the spacer plate 40 when the spacer plate 40 is fitted, is pressed against the selected workpiece edge 92, which fixes the setback. The sight notch 26 and the transverse center index 24 are then aligned with the centerline 94. Holding the jig in that position, the user drills through the drill bushing 54 of the first guide carriage 50 and through the drill bushing 74 of the second guide carriage 70. The jig is then lifted away from the workpiece 90. No part of the jig remains attached to the workpiece 90, and the workpiece 90 carries only the two guided holes and the pencil centerline 94.

When the next component of the run is to be referenced from the opposite edge, after the jig has been lifted away from the workpiece 90, the user removes the two screws 34, turns the fence 30 over, and reinstalls the two screws 34 in the two holes 36. The downwardly projecting portion 32 then stands on the opposite longitudinal side of the rail 10, the upper face 12 still faces the user, and the carriage settings and the reading direction of the distance marks 18 are unchanged. The jig is used on the second component in the same way as on the first.

The rail 10, the fence 30, the bodies of the first guide carriage 50 and the second guide carriage 70, the clamping shoe of each guide carriage, the spacer plate 40, the first pointer 62, the second pointer 82, and the clamp knob 58 and the clamp knob 78 may be made of rigid polymer or of metal. The drill bushing 54 and the drill bushing 74 are made of hardened metal.

The jig is a hand tool and is operated entirely by hand. It is held against the front face of the workpiece 90 by hand during the drilling, and no part of it remains attached to the workpiece 90. The jig has no motor, no battery, no electronic sensor, and no network connection. Every setting is made mechanically and is read directly from the distance marks 18, the transverse center index 24, and the sight notch 26.
