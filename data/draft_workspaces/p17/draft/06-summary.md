A drilling jig is disclosed that locates and guides both holes of a cabinet pull pattern in a single placement of a hand tool, without any powered measuring system.

The jig includes an elongated straight rail having a flat lower face that rests on the front face of a cabinet door or drawer front, an upper face, and a straight longitudinal slot extending through the rail from the upper face to the lower face. Distance marks are carried on the upper face alongside the longitudinal slot.

A fence is secured to a first end of the rail by two screws received in two corresponding holes. The fence has a portion that projects below the lower face and bears against a straight edge of the workpiece, and the fence is perpendicular to the rail so that the rail extends inward across the workpiece while the projecting portion bears against the edge. The fence is reversible. Removing the two screws, turning the fence over, and reinstalling the two screws moves the projecting portion from one longitudinal side of the rail to the opposite longitudinal side, while the marked upper face continues to face away from the workpiece. The same face of the rail, carrying the same distance marks reading in the same direction, is therefore used whether the tool is referenced from an upper edge or a lower edge of the workpiece, and the installer never has to read a second scale or work from an inverted tool.

Two separate guide carriages are seated on the upper face of the rail. Each guide carriage has a lower key that passes into the longitudinal slot, and the key prevents the guide carriage from rotating relative to the rail while allowing the guide carriage to slide along the longitudinal slot. Each guide carriage carries one cylindrical drill bushing having a bore that passes completely through the guide carriage and registers with the longitudinal slot, so that a drill bit passed through the bore reaches the workpiece. The two bores are parallel to one another and perpendicular to the flat lower face of the rail.

Each guide carriage has a threaded clamp knob above the rail. A threaded shank of the clamp knob passes through the guide carriage and the longitudinal slot into a clamping shoe below the rail. Turning the clamp knob draws the clamping shoe toward the guide carriage and clamps the rail between the guide carriage and the clamping shoe. Loosening the clamp knob allows the guide carriage to slide along the longitudinal slot.

The first guide carriage carries a first pointer aligned with the axis of its bore and readable against the distance marks, and the second guide carriage carries a second pointer aligned with the axis of its bore and readable against the distance marks. Each hole of the pair is therefore set by its own reading taken directly from the same scale, and the difference between the two pointer readings is the center-to-center hole spacing.

The rail has a transverse center index at its midpoint, and a shallow V-shaped sight notch is formed in a second end of the rail and aligned with the transverse center index. The transverse center index and the sight notch are aligned with a centerline marked on the workpiece. The pointer readings lie at equal distances on opposite sides of the transverse center index, so that the guided holes are centered about the transverse center index. The fence controls the setback of the holes from the straight edge of the workpiece, while the two pointer readings control the hole separation and the position of the pair relative to the centerline.

A removable spacer plate having parallel opposite faces may be fitted between the fence and the straight edge of the workpiece. The spacer plate changes the setback of every guided hole by its thickness without changing the separation between the holes, so that a second setback can be obtained without disturbing a spacing setting that has already been made.

In use, the installer selects the desired hole spacing, loosens both clamp knobs, slides the guide carriages until the pointer readings differ by the desired spacing and are centered about the transverse center index, tightens both clamp knobs, places the flat lower face on the workpiece with the fence or the installed spacer plate pressed against the selected edge, aligns the sight notch and the transverse center index with the centerline, and drills through both drill bushings. The jig is then lifted away, and no part of the jig remains attached to the workpiece.
