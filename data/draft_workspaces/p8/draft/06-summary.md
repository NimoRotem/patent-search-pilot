A vibration device is disclosed for bedding a covering element into thinset, mortar, adhesive or similar bonding material. The device has a rigid base with a first side and a second side. A vibration motor is coupled to the first side. An air-extraction mechanism is coupled to the first side and is in fluid communication with a chamber on the second side. A perimeter member extends from the second side at a perimeter of that side, runs around the chamber, and terminates in a bearing face that rests on the exposed face of the covering element. The perimeter member is rigid and of a low-friction material such as plastic, metal, polytetrafluoroethylene, or a combination of rigid materials, and the bearing face is the end face of that member.

The perimeter member is not a seal and is not asked to behave like one. When the bearing face rests on the exposed face, a clearance remains along at least part of the perimeter member through which ambient air passes continuously into the chamber. The perimeter member restricts that flow without stopping it. The air-extraction mechanism withdraws air from the chamber at a volumetric extraction rate sufficient to maintain the chamber below atmospheric pressure while ambient air continues to enter through the clearance. The resulting pressure differential acts over the area of the chamber and presses the device against the covering element.

Two consequences follow, and they are the point of the arrangement. First, the force holding the device down is supplied by the pressure differential rather than by the operator, so the operator need not press the device against the covering element, and the loaded, vibrating path from tool to hand is avoided rather than damped. Second, every member in the path from the vibration motor to the covering element, namely the base and the perimeter member terminating in the bearing face, is rigid, with no elastomeric or foam element interposed, so the vibration crosses into the covering element with substantially less damping than through a flexible suction cup. The device is thus coupled to the covering element without an intervening compliant member or air film, while the operator's hands carry less.

Because the member that touches the covering element is a rigid low-friction bearing face rather than a gripping cup, the device can be translated across the exposed face while the pressure differential is being maintained. Holding and sliding are not alternative modes to be switched between: the same continuously running extraction keeps the device down while it is moved, so vibration can be delivered at successive positions over the covering element without the hold ever being released and without the vibration intensity falling away with distance from a fixed attachment point.

In one embodiment the device carries one or more handles, which serve to guide and steer the device rather than to press it down. In another embodiment a flexible pulling element such as a rope, wire or cable is attached to the device, and the operator moves the device by pulling on that element. This gives decoupled control: the position of the device is set by a small pulling force applied by the operator, while the force holding the device against the covering element, and with it the quality of the vibration coupling, is set independently and constantly by the pressure differential.

In a further embodiment the device carries one or more stirring elements, and the vibration produced by the vibration motor, acting through those stirring elements, causes a constant forward motion of the device over the exposed face. The device then need not be pulled or guided by hand at all, the pressure differential continuing to hold it against the covering element throughout the movement.

A method of bedding a covering element using the device, and a system comprising the device together with the flexible pulling element, are also disclosed.
