In the following description, the same reference numeral denotes the same part throughout the drawings. The embodiments described are examples and do not limit the scope of what is claimed.

Overview

FIG. 1 shows a vibration device 10 in use, FIG. 2 shows it in vertical section, and FIG. 4 shows the bedded layers beneath it in enlarged detail. A covering element 36, which may be a tile or other covering material, has been laid onto a bed of bonding material 40 spread over a substrate 42, as seen in FIG. 4. The bonding material 40 may be thinset, mortar, tile adhesive or any similar bonding material. The substrate 42 is the surface being covered. The covering element 36 presents an upper face, hereinafter the exposed face 38, facing away from the bonding material 40. The vibration device 10 rests on the exposed face 38 and delivers vibration into the covering element 36, which drives trapped air out of the bonding material 40, distributes the bonding material 40 more evenly beneath the covering element 36, increases the area of contact between the covering element 36 and the bonding material 40, and strengthens the bond between the covering element 36 and the substrate 42.

The vibration device 10 comprises a base 12, a vibration motor 18, an air-extraction mechanism 20 and a perimeter member 24. The base 12 is rigid and has a first side 14 facing away from the covering element 36 and a second side 16 opposite the first side 14 and facing the covering element 36. The vibration motor 18 is coupled to the first side 14. The air-extraction mechanism 20 is also coupled to the first side 14 and is in fluid communication with a chamber 22 located on the second side 16. The perimeter member 24 extends from the second side 16 at a perimeter of the second side 16, runs around the chamber 22, and terminates in an end face, hereinafter the bearing face 26, that contacts the exposed face 38.

The base

The base 12 is rigid: under the alternating force produced by the vibration motor 18 and under the pressure differential described below, the base 12 is not compliant, and it transmits the vibration to the perimeter member 24 with substantially less damping than a flexible suction cup would.

The second side 16 of the base 12 forms the upper boundary of the chamber 22. The chamber 22 is in fluid communication with the air-extraction mechanism 20.

The vibration motor

The vibration motor 18 is coupled to the first side 14 of the base 12, with no elastomeric member, foam member or resilient mount interposed between the vibration motor 18 and the base 12. That direct coupling is intentional. A resilient motor mount would isolate the base 12 from the vibration motor 18, which is the opposite of what is wanted: the entire object of the device is to put vibration into the covering element 36, and any compliant member anywhere between the vibration motor 18 and the exposed face 38 removes energy from that transfer.

The vibration motor 18 produces mechanical vibration in the base 12.

The air-extraction mechanism

The air-extraction mechanism 20 is coupled to the first side 14 of the base 12, as seen in FIG. 2, and withdraws air from the chamber 22. In the embodiment shown, the air-extraction mechanism 20 is a vacuum pump carried on the base 12.

The air-extraction mechanism 20 runs continuously while the vibration device 10 is in use. It is not run to establish a vacuum and then stopped: the chamber 22 is never sealed, so the pressure differential exists only while the air-extraction mechanism 20 withdraws air from the chamber 22.

The perimeter member and the chamber

The perimeter member 24 stands proud of the second side 16 and surrounds the chamber 22: a rigid loop, or skirt, that runs round the second side 16 at its perimeter. As shown in FIG. 3, the perimeter member 24 is a closed loop running around the second side 16 at its perimeter. The perimeter member 24, the second side 16 of the base 12 and, in use, the exposed face 38 of the covering element 36 together bound the chamber 22. The chamber 22 is a single space bounded by one perimeter member 24, as shown in FIG. 2. The perimeter member 24 is rigidly connected to the base 12 at the second side 16, and vibration passes from the base 12 into the perimeter member 24 with substantially less damping than through a flexible suction cup.

The perimeter member 24 terminates, at its end remote from the base 12, in a bearing face 26 that, in use, bears on the exposed face 38 of the covering element 36. The perimeter member 24 is made of a low-friction rigid material, and the bearing face 26 is the end face of that member, so that the face bearing on the exposed face 38 is of that low-friction material. Suitable materials are plastic, metal, polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), and combinations of rigid materials.

The perimeter member 24 is not a seal. It is free of rubber, elastomer and foam. This distinguishes it from a suction cup and from a compliant sealing skirt, and the difference is functional rather than nominal. A suction cup works by conforming to the surface, closing the enclosed space against the atmosphere, and holding the reduced pressure inside; conforming requires compliance, and compliance in the load path damps vibration. The perimeter member 24 is instead rigid and does not conform to the exposed face 38, and it makes rigid contact with the exposed face 38.

Because the perimeter member 24 is not intended to form a complete airtight seal, a gap, hereinafter the clearance 34, remains between the bearing face 26 and the exposed face 38 along at least part of the perimeter member 24, as shown enlarged in FIG. 4. The clearance 34 is shown enlarged for clarity. FIG. 2 is a vertical sectional view showing the perimeter member 24 bearing on the covering element 36, and FIG. 4 is an enlarged fragmentary sectional view showing the clearance 34 that is present along at least part of the perimeter member 24. The enlarged detail of FIG. 4 also shows the exposed face 38 of the covering element 36, and the covering element 36 bedded in the bonding material 40 that has been spread over the substrate 42. Ambient air passes through the clearance 34 into the chamber 22 continuously while the vibration device 10 is in use. The clearance 34 is not a defect to be eliminated. It is the condition the vibration device 10 is designed to work in: the perimeter member 24 restricts the flow of ambient air into the chamber 22 without closing that path.

The pressure differential that holds the device down

The air-extraction mechanism 20 withdraws air from the chamber 22 at a volumetric extraction rate sufficient to maintain the chamber 22 below atmospheric pressure while ambient air continues to enter through the clearance 34, for as long as the air-extraction mechanism 20 runs.

The difference between atmospheric pressure and the pressure in the chamber 22, acting over the plan area of the chamber 22, produces a holding force that presses the vibration device 10 against the covering element 36. The holding force is sufficient to hold the vibration device 10 against the exposed face 38 without any downward force being applied by an operator. The contact load is therefore supplied by the pressure differential rather than by the operator's hands.

The vibration device 10 can be translated across the exposed face 38 while the pressure differential is being maintained. The pressure differential is maintained continuously throughout the translating, so that the vibration device 10 is held against the covering element 36 and slid over the exposed face 38 at the same time; holding and sliding are simultaneous, not alternatives. The bearing face 26, being of a low-friction material, is slidable over the exposed face 38 while the pressure differential is maintained, and the air-extraction mechanism 20 continues to run and continues to hold the vibration device 10 down throughout the movement. No grip has to be released and re-established. Vibration can therefore be delivered to successive areas of the covering element 36, instead of the vibration intensity falling away with distance from a single fixed attachment point.

The vibration transmission path

The vibration transmission path runs as follows. Vibration produced by the vibration motor 18 passes into the base 12 at the first side 14, along the base 12, into the perimeter member 24 at the second side 16, along the perimeter member 24, and out through the bearing face 26 into the exposed face 38 of the covering element 36. Every member in that path, namely the base 12 and the perimeter member 24 terminating in the bearing face 26, is rigid, and no elastomeric member, foam member or resilient mount is interposed anywhere along it. The vibration therefore reaches the covering element 36 with substantially less damping than it would in an arrangement in which a flexible suction cup, a resilient motor mount or a vibration-damping handle lies in the path.

The holding force from the pressure differential also acts to keep the bearing face 26 in contact with the exposed face 38. Contact is maintained by a force that does not vary with what the operator is doing, which is what makes the transfer consistent across the working area.

The clearance 34 does not separate the bearing face 26 from the covering element 36 over the whole of the loop, in the way that a film of air, where such a film remains, separates a pressed-on sliding base from the covering element beneath it. Where a film of that kind remains beneath a sliding base of that kind, it lies between that sliding base and the covering element beneath it, so that what crosses the interface crosses the film, and the film may dampen the vibration crossing it. Here the bearing face 26 is loaded onto the exposed face 38 by the holding force and bears rigidly on it, and the vibration is carried across that rigid contact. The clearance 34, present along at least part of the perimeter member 24, admits ambient air into the chamber 22 without the bearing face 26 being separated from the exposed face 38 by an intervening compliant member. Nothing compressible is interposed in the load path, and the holding force presses the contact together rather than holding the two faces apart.

The force balance on the vibration device 10 in use is as follows. The weight of the vibration device 10 and the holding force act downward on the covering element 36; the reaction of the exposed face 38 acts upward; the frictional resistance of the bearing face 26 acts in the plane of the exposed face 38 and opposes translation; and, where the vibration device 10 is moved by the operator, the force the operator need supply is an in-plane force sufficient to overcome that frictional resistance and to steer. Because no downward force is required from the operator, no loaded path need be maintained from the vibration device 10 into the operator's hands, wrists, arms and joints. The vibration reaching the operator is reduced not by absorbing it in a damper, which would take the same energy away from the covering element 36, but by removing the operator from the load path altogether. The usual trade-off, in which stronger coupling to the covering element 36 is bought with greater hand pressure and therefore with greater vibration exposure, does not arise here: the coupling is set by the air-extraction mechanism 20 and the operator's grip has nothing to do with it.

Handles

The vibration device 10 may include one or more handles 44, as shown in FIG. 1. The handle 44 is arranged to guide the vibration device 10 over the exposed face 38 while the pressure differential holds the vibration device 10 against the covering element 36. The handle 44 serves primarily to guide the vibration device 10 rather than to press it against the covering element 36, because the contact load is supplied by the pressure differential. The handle 44 is shown schematically in FIG. 1.

Embodiment with a flexible pulling element

FIG. 5 shows a second embodiment. A flexible pulling element 46 is connected to the vibration device 10. The flexible pulling element 46 is a rope, wire, cable or similar flexible pulling element. The vibration device 10 is translatable over the exposed face 38 by tension applied to the flexible pulling element 46 while the pressure differential is maintained: the operator applies that tension and the vibration device 10 slides on the bearing face 26 in the direction of the applied tension.

This embodiment gives decoupled control. The flexible pulling element 46 moves the vibration device 10 across the exposed face 38 when the operator applies tension to it. The force holding the vibration device 10 against the covering element 36 is supplied independently, and continuously, by the pressure differential across the base 12. Position is thus controlled by one agency and contact by another: the operator sets where the vibration device 10 is with a small pulling force, while the quality of the vibration coupling is set by the air-extraction mechanism 20 and is supplied independently of the tension applied to the flexible pulling element 46.

Embodiment with stirring elements

A third embodiment, shown schematically in FIG. 6, provides for autonomous movement. The vibration device 10 carries one or more stirring elements 48. The vibration produced by the vibration motor 18, acting through the one or more stirring elements 48, causes a constant forward motion of the vibration device 10 over the exposed face 38, so that the vibration device 10 need not be pulled or guided by hand, the pressure differential continuing to hold the vibration device 10 against the covering element 36 throughout the movement.

Method of use

A method of bedding a covering element 36 into bonding material 40 using the vibration device 10 is carried out as follows. The method begins with placing the vibration device 10 on the exposed face 38 of the covering element 36, whereby the bearing face 26 of the perimeter member 24 is brought against the exposed face 38 so that the chamber 22 is bounded by the second side 16, the perimeter member 24 and the exposed face 38, with the clearance 34 remaining, along at least part of the perimeter member 24, between the bearing face 26 and the exposed face 38. It continues with operating the air-extraction mechanism 20 to withdraw air from the chamber 22 at a volumetric extraction rate sufficient to maintain the chamber 22 below atmospheric pressure while ambient air continues to enter the chamber 22 through the clearance 34, the resulting pressure differential holding the vibration device 10 against the covering element 36 without a downward force being required from the operator. It continues with operating the vibration motor 18 and transmitting vibration from the vibration motor 18 to the covering element 36 through the base 12 and the perimeter member 24 to the bearing face 26. It continues with translating the vibration device 10 across the exposed face 38, while the pressure differential is maintained, by sliding the bearing face 26 over the exposed face 38, so that vibration is delivered to successive areas of the covering element 36. The translating may be done by applying tension to the flexible pulling element 46 or by guiding the vibration device 10 with the handle 44.
