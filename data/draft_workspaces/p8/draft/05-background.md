When a tile or similar covering element is laid onto a bed of thinset, mortar or adhesive, air is trapped between the covering element and the bonding material and the bonding material is unevenly distributed beneath the covering element. Mechanical vibration applied to the exposed face of the covering element after it is laid drives the trapped air out, spreads the bonding material into a more uniform layer, increases the area over which the bonding material actually touches the covering element, and thereby improves the strength of the completed bond. Hand-guided vibration devices are used for this purpose.

Such devices generally comprise a vibration motor carried on a handle together with one of two arrangements for putting the vibration into the covering element: a smooth sliding base that the operator presses down onto the exposed face, or a flexible suction cup that grips the exposed face at one place. In one known hand-held device for lifting and laying tiles, a handle carries two shafts, an electric motor vibrates the shafts, and a head shell at the free end of each shaft carries a suction cup on its underside, the suction cups being evacuated by an electrically operated pump and each being formed by a rubber-elastic apron [REF:DE-202021002652-U1].

The first of two problems with existing tile vibration devices is that both arrangements couple the vibration into the covering element poorly. A smooth sliding base has to be pressed hard against the covering element by the operator, and even under that pressure a thin film of air can remain between that sliding base and the covering element and damp the vibration crossing the interface. A flexible suction cup makes a firmer connection, but the rubber or foam wall that forms the cup is compliant, and a compliant member placed in the path between the motor and the covering element absorbs and dissipates part of the vibration instead of delivering it. A device gripped by such a cup is also anchored at one place on the covering element: it cannot be slid to another area without first releasing the grip, and the vibration intensity reaching areas remote from the point of attachment falls off with distance from that point.

Vacuum coupling arrangements used away from tile setting approach the holding of a body against a surface in other ways, each on the terms of its own document. In one adhesion device that holds itself to a wall surface, the peripheral wall of the decompressed space between the base and the wall surface is formed by elastic partitions, of which the partition nearer the wall surface has an end that contacts the wall surface, and a frame member of higher rigidity is added on the partition to restrict the partition from being deformed into that space as it is drawn down [REF:US-11408462-B2]. In a base assembly for coupling an end effector to a structure, a cavity on the second side of the assembly is held to the structure by vacuum drawn by a pneumatic generator operating in a suction mode, and the assembly is moved across the structure substantially frictionlessly on an air cushion produced by the same generator operating in a blower mode, a control being provided that selectively switches the generator between the suction mode and the blower mode [REF:US-10001245-B2]. In a device that suction-adheres to a wall surface and moves along it, a seal member mounted in the main body defines a pressure reduction space in cooperation with the wall surface, vacuum creating means discharges fluid from that space so that the main body adheres by the difference in fluid pressure inside and outside the space, and vibration generating means mounted on the main body imparts a lifting force in a direction away from the wall surface that reduces the suction-adhering force, together with a force along the wall surface that moves the main body [REF:US-4934475-A]. The vibration there is used to loosen the suction-adhering hold and to drive the main body along the wall surface.

Vacuum is used differently again in handling equipment, where the object is to pick a workpiece up rather than to hold a tool down on it: in one vacuum plate lifting system for lifting sheet material articles, a substantially horizontal suction plate has a perforated suction lifting surface directed downward for contact with the upper surface of the article, an airflow suction system conveys vacuum air pressure to that surface, a lifter actuation system such as a robotic arm lifts and moves the plate, and an elongate bar movably mounted along an edge of the plate carries nozzles that are lowered to sweep a suction airflow across the surface beneath to remove dust or debris [REF:US-11247858-B2]. That system carries the article away from the work surface and cleans the work surface by suction; it does not deliver vibration into a covering element already laid on a bed of bonding material, and it accordingly says nothing about how a vibrating tool might be kept in contact with such a covering element while it is moved over it.

The second problem with existing tile vibration devices concerns the operator rather than the tile. Because contact has to be maintained by hand, the operator applies substantial and continuous downward force through the handle, and vibration travels back up that loaded path into the hands, wrists, arms and joints, producing discomfort, fatigue and a risk of injury. Shock absorbers and vibration-damping handles reduce some of the vibration reaching the operator, but they do so by absorbing mechanical energy that would otherwise have gone into the covering element, so the tool is made kinder to the operator by being made worse at its job. The two problems are coupled: firmer coupling to the covering element is obtained by pressing harder, and pressing harder is exactly what increases the vibration dose to the operator.

What is needed is a way to hold a vibration device against a covering element with a force that does not come from the operator's arms, to conduct the vibration into the covering element without passing it through any compliant member, and to allow the device to be moved from area to area over the covering element without ever giving up that hold.
