FIG. 1 shows a charging installation 100 in which several electric vehicles are charged from one electrical branch. A branch conductor 102 is the conductor of that electrical branch and has a branch current limit, that is, an allowable aggregate current of the branch conductor 102, and a reference below to the electrical branch is a reference to the branch supplied by the branch conductor 102. The branch current limit is lower than a sum of the current ratings of the vehicle connectors served by the branch conductor 102, so that the connectors cannot all deliver their rated current at the same time and the aggregate current is governed by control rather than by the rating of any one connector.

A first connector channel 120 and a second connector channel 140 are connected to the branch conductor 102 in parallel. The description of the first connector channel 120 applies to each further connector channel connected to the branch conductor 102, the second connector channel 140 being of the same construction. A non-charging load 118 is also connected to the branch conductor 102 and represents the equipment of the site that shares the branch and whose current the controller does not command.

A branch current sensor 104 is arranged on the branch conductor 102 upstream of the contactors of the connector channels and measures a branch current, that is, the total current carried by the branch conductor 102. Because the branch current sensor 104 is upstream of the connector channels, its measurement of the branch current is independent of the measurements made within the connector channels. The branch current sensor 104 is treated as an independent safety input throughout this description.

An edge controller 106, also referred to herein as a controller, is coupled to the branch current sensor 104 and is coupled to the connector channels by an isolated local bus 108. The isolated local bus 108 carries communications between the edge controller 106 and the connector channels, including the assigned current limits, the contactor commands and the pilot reports.

FIG. 2 shows the first connector channel 120 in more detail. A contactor 122 is connected between the branch conductor 102 of FIG. 1 and a vehicle connector 128. The contactor 122 is controllable by the edge controller 106 and, when open, isolates the vehicle connector 128 from the branch. A connector current sensor 124 is arranged to measure a connector current delivered through the vehicle connector 128. A control-pilot interface 126 exchanges control pilot signalling with an electric vehicle 130 that is coupled to the vehicle connector 128. The control-pilot interface 126 signals a current limit to the electric vehicle 130 and reports the presence of a connected vehicle at the vehicle connector 128. The isolated local bus 108 couples the edge controller 106 to the contactor 122 and the control-pilot interface 126 of the first connector channel 120, and the edge controller 106 is also coupled to the connector current sensor 124. The first connector channel 120 is associated with a local connector identifier, which the signed local authorization token names when it authorizes that connector channel.

The current limit signalled through the control-pilot interface 126 is a request addressed to the electric vehicle 130. The electric vehicle 130 governs the current it actually draws. The connector current sensor 124 therefore measures what the connector channel is actually delivering, and the branch current sensor 104 measures what the branch is actually carrying, and neither measurement depends on the cooperation of the electric vehicle 130.

FIG. 3 shows the edge controller 106 and the local fault indicator 116 that it operates. The edge controller 106 stores, for each active connector channel, a charging authorization, a requested current, a minimum sustaining current and an energy-deficit value. The charging authorization records that the session at that connector channel is permitted to draw current. The requested current is the current requested for the session at that connector channel. The minimum sustaining current is the lowest current at which the session is to be maintained. The energy-deficit value is a value stored for the session and used in distributing the remainder of the available charging current among the active connector channels.

A local fault indicator 116 at the installation is coupled to the edge controller 106 and is operated by the edge controller 106 to signal a condition that requires attention at the equipment itself. Connections leave the edge controller 106 and lead to the isolated local bus 108 and to the branch current sensor 104.

FIG. 4 shows the operation of the edge controller 106 over a succession of allocation intervals, and the protective actions that follow when the branch does not respond to the current limits that have been signalled.

At an available current determination step 202, performed at each allocation interval, the edge controller 106 subtracts a measured non-charging branch load and a configurable safety reserve from the branch current limit to obtain an available charging current. The measured non-charging branch load is the current on the branch that the edge controller 106 does not command. The configurable safety reserve is a stored margin that keeps the commanded charging current below the branch current limit. Because the non-charging branch load is measured at each allocation interval rather than assumed, the available charging current follows the spare capacity of the branch.

At a sustaining and deficit assignment step 204, the edge controller 106 first assigns the minimum sustaining current from the available charging current to each active connector channel that can receive the minimum sustaining current, and then distributes a remainder of the available charging current among the active connector channels according to the stored energy-deficit values, thereby obtaining an assigned current limit for each active connector channel. Assigning the minimum sustaining currents first ensures that the capacity of the branch is not divided so finely that every session is left below the current at which it can be sustained.

At a pilot command step 206, the edge controller 106 sends the assigned current limit for each active connector channel through the control-pilot interface of that connector channel, over the isolated local bus 108.

At a connector verification step 208, the edge controller 106 verifies the connector current resulting from the assigned current limit with the connector current sensor of that connector channel. The verification compares the measured connector current with the assigned current limit for that connector channel.

After the connector verification step 208, at a branch overcurrent detection step 209, the edge controller 106 compares the measured branch current from the branch current sensor 104 with the branch current limit. So long as the measured branch current is below the branch current limit, the process returns to the available current determination step 202 at the next allocation interval. When the measured branch current is above the branch current limit, the process proceeds to a reduced pilot command sending step 210.

At the reduced pilot command sending step 210, the edge controller 106 sends a reduced pilot command.

After the reduced pilot command has been sent, at an overcurrent persistence verification step 211, the edge controller 106 determines if the measured branch current remains above the branch current limit for a verification interval. If the measured branch current does not remain above the branch current limit for the verification interval, the process returns to the available current determination step 202. If the measured branch current does remain above the branch current limit for the verification interval, the process proceeds to an ordered shedding step 212.

At the ordered shedding step 212, responsive to the determination at the overcurrent persistence verification step 211, the edge controller 106 opens the contactors of the connector channels one at a time in a stored shedding order until the measured branch current is below the branch current limit. The stored shedding order lists the connector channels of the branch in the sequence in which they are to be removed. After each contactor is opened, the edge controller 106 reads the branch current sensor 104 again, and it opens no further contactor once the measured branch current is below the branch current limit. Load is therefore removed in a known sequence, in the smallest number of steps that the measured branch current shows to be necessary. Ordered shedding under measurement removes as few connector channels as the measurement requires, in the stored shedding order, with the remaining connector channels still charging.

The two current measurements do different work, and the combination of them is what allows the reduction to be staged. The connector current sensor of a connector channel shows whether that connector channel is drawing no more than its assigned current limit. The branch current sensor 104 states whether the branch as a whole is still above its limit, whatever the connector channels report, and is the input on which the opening of a contactor at the ordered shedding step 212 depends. A measured branch current that remains above the branch current limit for the verification interval after the reduced pilot command has been sent, as determined at the overcurrent persistence verification step 211, therefore causes a contactor to be opened at the ordered shedding step 212, and no contactor is opened at that step while the measured branch current is below the branch current limit.

At a welded-contactor detection step 214, the edge controller 106 determines if a welded-contactor condition exists by testing whether the connector current of a connector channel remains above a leakage threshold after an open command has been issued to the contactor of that connector channel. On recording the welded-contactor condition, the edge controller 106 inhibits the contactors of the other connector channels on the electrical branch, so that no further connector channel is closed onto a branch that already carries an uncontrollable load, and operates the local fault indicator 116 at the installation, so that the condition is signalled where the equipment stands. While the welded-contactor condition stands, the inhibited contactors are not reclosed.

At a conditional reclosure step 216, reached where no welded-contactor condition has been recorded on the electrical branch, the edge controller 106 does not reclose the contactor of a connector channel that has been opened until three conditions are satisfied together. The connector current sensor of that connector channel reports zero connector current, which confirms that the contactor did in fact open and distinguishes a successfully shed connector channel from a welded one. The control-pilot interface of that connector channel reports a connected vehicle, so that a contactor is not closed onto a connector with no vehicle at it. A randomized recovery delay has expired, so that connector channels that were shed together do not reclose together and return the branch immediately to the condition that caused the shedding. Reclosure of the contactor is withheld until the three conditions are satisfied together, and a connector channel whose contactor has been reclosed takes part in the allocation at the next allocation interval.
