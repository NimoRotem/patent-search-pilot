TITLE OF THE INVENTION

Self-Centering Split Pipe Clamp With Synchronized Radial Jaws

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0003] The present disclosure relates to clamps that grip the outside of a pipe, and more particularly to a reusable split clamp whose frame opens on a hinge so that the clamp can be placed around a pipe from the side, and whose radially movable jaws are advanced together onto the pipe by a single latch motion.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[0004] Equipment is frequently attached to the outside of a pipe after the pipe has been installed. Sensors, small tools, brackets, and lengths of conduit all have to be carried on a pipe that cannot be taken out of service, cannot be lifted, and has no free end over which a closed ring could be threaded. A clamp intended for that duty therefore has to open from the side, close around the pipe, and grip it firmly enough that the carried equipment keeps its position, while remaining removable so that the same clamp can be taken off and used again elsewhere.
[0005] A clamp that opens from the side is split into parts, and the split is what makes the gripping problem difficult. Once the clamp body is divided, any drive element that runs around the body is divided with it. If the gripping surfaces of each part are driven separately, the fixed relationship between the two parts of the drive is given up: the two sides of the clamp advance by amounts that depend on how each side is operated, so the clamp tends to settle off center on the pipe, and the load is shared unevenly between the gripping surfaces. If the split body is instead pulled closed on the pipe, the closing force passes through the joint at the split and the pipe itself reacts the closing motion, so the grip depends on how nearly the bore of the clamp matches the outside diameter of the pipe, and a part dimensioned to a particular pipe diameter is needed, so that a different pipe size calls for a different part.
[0006] Reusable clamping also has to deal with the force the operator applies. A hand lever gives the operator no measure of what has been done to the pipe. When the gripping elements have reached the pipe, further travel of the lever has to go somewhere, and in a rigid drive train it goes into whichever gripping element happens to touch first, so that one gripping surface is loaded far more than the others. Concentrated loading of a single gripping surface is undesirable, and the operator has no indication that it has occurred. An added instrument that responds to the position of the gripping elements reports the size of the pipe rather than the force in the grip.
[0007] What has been lacking is a split clamp in which the drive that advances the gripping elements is itself coupled by the act of closing the clamp into a single element that moves as one ring, in which every gripping element is advanced from that one drive so that the gripping elements advance together over a range of pipe sizes, and in which the travel remaining in the operator's lever after the gripping elements have landed on the pipe is absorbed within the clamp and reported to the operator as a loading condition rather than as a dimension.

BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[0008] A split pipe clamp is disclosed in which the element that advances the gripping jaws is divided with the clamp body but is coupled by the act of closing the clamp into a single element that moves as one ring, so that one latch motion advances every jaw together.
[0009] In one aspect, a split pipe clamp comprises a first frame half and a second frame half joined at a hinge end by a hinge and releasably joined at a latch end by a latch. In a closed condition the two frame halves define a frame having an annular guide and three radial guides. Three jaw carriages are each slidable radially in a respective one of the radial guides, and each jaw carriage carries a jaw pad that is pivotally mounted on the jaw carriage and has a concave bearing face directed inwardly of the frame. A segmented cam ring is received in the annular guide and comprises a first cam ring segment carried by the first frame half and a second cam ring segment carried by the second frame half. The cam ring segments have complementary coupling faces at the hinge end and complementary coupling faces at the latch end, and those faces engage one another as the frame halves are closed, so that in the closed condition the two segments move in the annular guide as a coupled ring. Each jaw carriage has a follower received in a respective oblique slot of the segmented cam ring, each oblique slot being inclined to a radial direction of the frame so that rotation of the coupled ring through a stroke advances the three jaw carriages inwardly in synchronism. A wedge movable by the latch bears on a ring drive face of the segmented cam ring, so that movement of the latch rotates the coupled ring through the stroke, and a carriage return spring biases each jaw carriage outwardly so that releasing the latch retracts the jaws.
[0010] Because the coupled ring is a single kinematic element spanning both frame halves, the three jaw carriages are not driven independently. One latch motion, applied at one point on the cam ring, is distributed by the coupled ring to every jaw carriage, and the jaw pads close on the pipe together. The pivoting concave jaw pads seat on the pipe without a part dimensioned to a particular pipe diameter.
[0011] In a further aspect, the latch has a first position in which the frame halves are held around the pipe without clamping and a second, over-center position in which the wedge drives the cam ring and the pipe is clamped, and a force-limiting spring stack lies in the drive train between a latch handle and the wedge. Travel of the handle that remains after all three jaw pads have contacted the pipe compresses the force-limiting spring stack instead of advancing one jaw carriage farther than the others. An indicator pin moves relative to a fixed edge of a latch housing when the force-limiting spring stack enters a predetermined compression range, giving the operator a mechanical report of clamp loading that is produced by the state of the spring stack and not by the diameter of the pipe.
[0012] In a further aspect, the frame carries a mounting boss outside the annular guide for supporting a sensor, a tool, or a conduit, the mounting boss not participating in the advance of the three jaw carriages inwardly in synchronism, so that equipment carried on the frame is outside the path by which the jaws are synchronized.
[0013] In a further aspect, a method of clamping a pipe comprises opening the latch, swinging the frame halves apart about the hinge, placing the frame halves around a pipe, closing the frame halves so that the complementary coupling faces engage and couple the cam ring segments, moving the latch to the first position, and moving the latch to the second, over-center position so that the wedge rotates the coupled ring and the followers advance the three jaw carriages inwardly in synchronism until the jaw pads seat on the pipe. Releasing the latch unloads the force-limiting spring stack and the carriage return springs retract the jaw carriages before the frame halves are opened.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

[0020] The following description of embodiments refers to the accompanying drawings. The same reference numeral denotes the same part throughout. Terms of direction are used with respect to the frame of the clamp: the axis of the frame is the axis about which the frame is annular in the closed condition, "inward" and "radially inward" mean toward that axis, "outward" and "radially outward" mean away from it, and "circumferential" means about that axis. These terms describe relationships within the clamp and do not require the clamp to be used in any particular attitude.
[0021] Overall arrangement
[0022] FIG. 1 shows a split pipe clamp 100 closed around a pipe 90, with the latch 16 in the over-center position described below and the jaw pads 40 seated on the pipe 90. The clamp 100 has a first frame half 10 and a second frame half 12. Each of the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 is semicircular, and the two frame halves together surround the pipe 90. The first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 are joined at one end, referred to as the hinge end, by a hinge 14, and are releasably joined at the other end, referred to as the latch end, by a releasable latch 16. The hinge 14 permits the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 to be swung apart far enough for the frame halves to be placed around the pipe 90 from the side. When the frame halves are brought together and the latch 16 is engaged, the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 define a frame that is annular about the axis of the frame.
[0023] Three jaw carriages 30 are distributed around the frame in the closed condition, so that the three jaw carriages 30 approach the pipe 90 from three different directions. Each jaw carriage 30 moves radially with respect to the frame and carries at its inner end a jaw pad 40, which is described below in connection with FIG. 3. The jaw pads 40 carried at the inner ends of the three jaw carriages 30 bear on the pipe 90 when the clamp 100 is clamped. The clamp 100 does not require a part dimensioned to a particular pipe diameter, and the same clamp 100 serves pipes of different diameters within the radial travel of the jaw carriages 30.
[0024] The frame may carry a mounting boss 70 outside the annular guide 18 described below, as shown in FIG. 5, which is a fragmentary view with parts shown in section on a plane containing the axis of the frame and corresponding in orientation to the plane of FIG. 3, that plane passing through the mounting boss 70. The mounting boss 70 supports equipment 80 that is to be held on the pipe 90 by the clamp 100. FIG. 5 shows the equipment 80 supported on the mounting boss 70, the equipment 80 being a sensor, a tool, or a conduit. The mounting boss 70 does not participate in the advance of the three jaw carriages 30 inwardly in synchronism. FIG. 5 also shows the annular guide 18 of the frame with the segmented cam ring 20 received in it, the mounting boss 70 standing outside the annular guide 18, no part of the mounting boss 70 reaching into the annular guide 18. The equipment 80 carried at the mounting boss 70 therefore lies outside the drive path by which the jaws are advanced. The mounting boss 70 is an optional feature of the clamp 100 and may be omitted.
[0025] The segmented cam ring
[0026] FIG. 2 shows the segmented cam ring 20, drawn removed from the frame, its segments being in the relative positions they occupy when coupled in the closed condition. The segmented cam ring 20 comprises a first cam ring segment 22 and a second cam ring segment 24. The first cam ring segment 22 is carried by the first frame half 10 and the second cam ring segment 24 is carried by the second frame half 12, so that each frame half carries one segment of the ring and each segment travels with its own frame half when the clamp 100 is opened.
[0027] The first cam ring segment 22 and the second cam ring segment 24 meet at two places, one at the hinge end and one at the latch end. At the hinge end the two segments have complementary coupling faces 26, and at the latch end they have complementary coupling faces 28. The complementary coupling faces 26 and the complementary coupling faces 28 are each shaped so that one segment can transmit circumferential force to the other through the joint. The faces are brought into engagement by the same motion that closes the frame halves: as the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 are swung together about the hinge 14, the complementary coupling faces 26 close on one another at the hinge end and the complementary coupling faces 28 close on one another at the latch end. FIG. 6 shows the joint at the latch end in the closed condition, the end of the first cam ring segment 22 held by the first frame half 10 and the end of the second cam ring segment 24 held by the second frame half 12, the two ends meeting at the complementary coupling faces 28.
[0028] Coupling the segments in this way solves a problem created by splitting the clamp. Here the division of the ring is undone by the act of closing the clamp. In the closed condition the first cam ring segment 22 and the second cam ring segment 24 are coupled at both ends and rotate together in the annular guide as a coupled ring, and a circumferential input applied to either segment is carried around the whole ring. One input, applied at one point on the cam ring, therefore reaches every jaw carriage 30 through a single kinematic element, and the relationship between the jaw carriages 30 is set by the geometry of the ring rather than by the way the clamp is operated. When the latch 16 is released and the frame halves are swung apart, the complementary coupling faces 26 and the complementary coupling faces 28 separate and each segment stays with its own frame half, so that the coupling is made and broken by the closing and opening of the frame halves.
[0029] The annular guide 18 retains the first cam ring segment 22 on the first frame half 10 and the second cam ring segment 24 on the second frame half 12, and permits circumferential movement of the coupled ring. Each segment is therefore held on its own frame half whether the clamp 100 is open or closed, and no separate operation is needed to place the ring in the frame when the frame halves are brought together.
[0030] The segmented cam ring 20 has three oblique slots 34, one for each jaw carriage 30. Each oblique slot 34 is inclined to the radial direction of the frame at the circumferential position of that slot, so that rotation of the coupled ring produces inward travel of its jaw carriage 30, the three oblique slots 34 being arranged so that the three jaw carriages 30 travel inwardly in synchronism.
[0031] The segmented cam ring 20 further has a ring drive face 36, shown in FIG. 4, against which the latch 16 acts through a wedge 52 as described below. The ring drive face 36 is presented at a position on the ring that the wedge 52 can reach, so that advance of the wedge 52 against the ring drive face 36 moves the segmented cam ring 20 circumferentially.
[0032] The jaw carriages
[0033] FIG. 3 is a fragmentary sectional view through one jaw carriage 30, taken on line 3-3 of FIG. 1. The frame is formed with the annular guide 18, which receives the segmented cam ring 20 and holds it against radial movement while leaving it free to move circumferentially.
[0034] The frame is further formed with a radial guide 38 at the position of each jaw carriage 30, so that the frame in the closed condition has the annular guide 18 and three radial guides 38. Each jaw carriage 30 is received in a respective one of the three radial guides 38 and is constrained by the radial guide 38 to move radially with respect to the frame. Each jaw carriage 30 carries a follower 32 that projects from the jaw carriage 30 into a respective one of the oblique slots 34 of the segmented cam ring 20. The follower 32 bears against the sides of the oblique slot 34.
[0035] The follower 32 is constrained in two ways at once. The radial guide 38 prevents the jaw carriage 30, and therefore the follower 32, from moving circumferentially, while the oblique slot 34 holds the follower 32 at a position along the slot, which is inclined to the radial direction, so that circumferential movement of the segmented cam ring 20 displaces the follower 32 radially. When the coupled ring turns through its stroke, the side of each oblique slot 34 bears on the corresponding follower 32 and drives it, and with it the jaw carriage 30, radially inward along the radial guide 38. Because all three followers 32 are engaged in oblique slots 34 of one coupled ring, the three jaw carriages 30 advance inwardly in synchronism for as long as the ring turns. The jaw pads 40 therefore close on the pipe together.
[0036] At its inner end each jaw carriage 30 carries a jaw pad 40. The jaw pad 40 is pivotally mounted on the jaw carriage 30 and has a concave bearing face directed inwardly of the frame. Because the jaw pad 40 is free to pivot on the jaw carriage 30, the concave bearing face aligns itself with the outer surface of the pipe as it lands. The same concave bearing face therefore seats on pipes of different diameters, and no part dimensioned to a particular pipe diameter is required.
[0037] A carriage return spring 44 acts on each jaw carriage 30 so as to urge that jaw carriage 30 radially outward. When the latch 16 is released and the drive from the latch to the ring drive face 36 is removed, the carriage return springs 44 push the jaw carriages 30 outward and the jaw pads 40 are retracted from the pipe, and the clamp 100 is left with its jaw carriages 30 retracted and ready for the next installation. Retraction of the jaw carriages 30 clears the jaw pads 40 from the pipe before the frame halves are swung apart.
[0038] The latch and the force-limiting drive train
[0039] The latch 16 comprises a latch housing 58 at the latch end, a latch handle 50, and a force-limiting spring stack 54 in a drive train between the latch handle 50 and the wedge 52, the wedge 52 being movable by the latch 16. The latch housing 58 presents a fixed edge referred to below in connection with the indicator pin 56. The wedge 52 is positioned to bear on the ring drive face 36 of the segmented cam ring 20.
[0040] FIG. 4 is an enlarged fragmentary view of the latch end, with parts shown in section, and shows the latch housing 58, the latch handle 50, the wedge 52, the force-limiting spring stack 54 and the indicator pin 56 in their relation to the first cam ring segment 22, the second cam ring segment 24 and the ring drive face 36.
[0041] The latch 16 has two engaged positions. In a first position, the latch 16 holds the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 closed around the pipe 90 but does not clamp it: the frame is captive on the pipe 90 and cannot fall away, while the segmented cam ring 20 has not yet advanced the jaw carriages 30. In a second, over-center position, the wedge 52 drives the segmented cam ring 20 and the pipe 90 is clamped. Movement of the latch handle 50 from the first position to the second position advances the wedge 52 against the ring drive face 36. That advance moves the segmented cam ring 20 circumferentially, and the coupled ring turns through its limited stroke.
[0042] The force-limiting spring stack 54 answers the problem of what becomes of the operator's remaining handle travel. The jaw pads 40 land on the pipe 90 at some point in the stroke that depends on the diameter of the pipe 90, and the latch handle 50 has still to be carried into its over-center position. If the drive train from the latch handle 50 to the wedge 52 were rigid, that remaining travel would be taken up by whatever part of the assembly yielded first, in practice by driving one jaw farther into the pipe 90 than the others and loading it far more heavily. Additional travel of the latch handle 50 after all three jaw pads 40 have contacted the pipe 90 compresses the force-limiting spring stack 54 rather than forcing one jaw carriage 30 farther inward than the others.
[0043] The indicator pin 56 is coupled to the force-limiting spring stack 54 and projects from the latch housing 58. As the force-limiting spring stack 54 compresses, the indicator pin 56 moves relative to a fixed edge of the latch housing 58, and the position of the indicator pin 56 with respect to that fixed edge shows the operator when the force-limiting spring stack 54 has entered a predetermined compression range. The indicator pin 56 therefore reports clamp loading mechanically, and what it reports is the state of the force-limiting spring stack 54. The indicator pin 56 is displaced by compression of the force-limiting spring stack 54 independently of a diameter of the pipe 90, and its position is not a measurement of that diameter.
[0044] Operation
[0045] To install the clamp 100 on a pipe 90, the operator opens the latch 16 and swings the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 apart about the hinge 14. The first cam ring segment 22 stays with the first frame half 10 and the second cam ring segment 24 stays with the second frame half 12, and the complementary coupling faces 26 and the complementary coupling faces 28 are separated. The operator places the frame halves around the pipe 90 from the side and closes them about the hinge 14. Closing the frame halves brings the complementary coupling faces 26 into engagement at the hinge end and the complementary coupling faces 28 into engagement at the latch end, coupling the first cam ring segment 22 and the second cam ring segment 24 into the coupled ring within the annular guide 18.
[0046] The operator then moves the latch 16 to its first position, in which the frame is held around the pipe 90 without clamping. The operator moves the latch handle 50 to the second, over-center position. The wedge 52 is driven against the ring drive face 36, the coupled ring turns through its stroke, the sides of the three oblique slots 34 drive the three followers 32, and the three jaw carriages 30 advance inwardly in synchronism until the pivoting jaw pads 40 seat on the pipe 90. The remaining travel of the latch handle 50 compresses the force-limiting spring stack 54, and the operator watches the indicator pin 56 move relative to the fixed edge of the latch housing 58 and so sees that the force-limiting spring stack 54 has entered the predetermined compression range. The latch 16 is left in the over-center position and the clamp 100 is loaded on the pipe 90.
[0047] To remove the clamp 100, the operator releases the latch 16. The latch handle 50 comes back over center, the wedge 52 retreats from the ring drive face 36, and the force-limiting spring stack 54 is unloaded. The carriage return springs 44 then push the jaw carriages 30 radially outward, so that the jaw pads 40 lift from the pipe 90. Only then are the first frame half 10 and the second frame half 12 swung apart about the hinge 14 and the clamp 100 taken off the pipe 90. The clamp 100 may be installed again on the same pipe or on a pipe of a different diameter within the radial travel of the jaw carriages 30.

ABSTRACT OF THE DISCLOSURE

A split pipe clamp has two frame halves joined by a hinge at one end and a latch at the other. A segmented cam ring lies in an annular guide of the frame, one segment carried by each half. Closing the halves engages complementary coupling faces at the hinge end and at the latch end, so the segments turn as one coupled ring. Three jaw carriages slide in radial guides, each having a follower in an oblique slot of the ring. Latch movement drives a wedge against a ring drive face and rotates the ring, advancing all three carriages inwardly in synchronism until pivoting concave jaw pads seat on the pipe. A force-limiting spring stack between the latch handle and the wedge absorbs remaining handle travel, an indicator pin reports its compression, and springs retract the jaws when the latch is released.
