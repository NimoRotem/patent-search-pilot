TITLE OF THE INVENTION

SPLIT PIPE CLAMP HAVING A SEGMENTED CAM RING COUPLED BY FRAME CLOSURE AND A FORCE LIMITED LATCH DRIVE

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0003] The present disclosure relates to clamps that are applied around a pipe, and more particularly to a reusable split clamp whose frame opens on a hinge to receive a pipe that is already in place and whose jaws are advanced onto the pipe by a latch driven cam mechanism.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

[0004] Pipework in process plants, buildings, vehicles and drilling installations often has to be gripped by a device that cannot be threaded on from a free end of the pipe. Sensors, monitoring heads, marking tools, small actuators and conduit supports are attached to a run of pipe that is already installed. A clamp intended for that duty is therefore built as a split clamp: a frame that opens, is placed around the pipe transversely, and is closed again.
[0005] A split frame presents a mechanical problem that a one piece collar does not have, because the tightening motion has to be delivered to a frame that is divided. Where the grip is obtained by drawing the two frame halves toward one another so that the frame itself shrinks onto the pipe, the closing force enters the frame at the split line, and the grip that results depends on the closure hardware and on how far the halves have been drawn together.
[0006] Where instead the frame is fitted with jaws that are advanced against the pipe one at a time, the grip is taken away from the split line, but the jaws have to be balanced against one another by the operator. If one jaw is advanced further than the others, the pipe is pushed away from the axis of the frame, and the clamp is seated differently each time it is fitted.
[0007] A clamp whose jaw faces match one nominal pipe diameter also obliges a technician to select the clamp to suit the pipe before working, and a jaw face of fixed curvature meets a pipe of a different diameter along an edge rather than over an area.
[0008] An operator working with a quick acting latch further has little indication of how hard the clamp has been applied. The latch closes to the same position whether the jaws have only just reached the pipe or have been driven hard against it, so the feel of the latch does not report the load the clamp is carrying, and a reading of pipe diameter reports only the size of the pipe.
[0009] There is accordingly a need for a split pipe clamp that can be fitted by hand around an in place pipe over a range of diameters, that advances several jaws onto the pipe together rather than one at a time, and that reports mechanically to the operator when the clamping load has reached the intended range.

BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

[0010] A reusable split pipe clamp is disclosed in which the ring that drives the jaws is itself split, one segment traveling with each half of the frame, and is made into a working ring by the act of closing the frame.
[0011] The clamp has a first frame half and a second frame half joined at a hinge end by a hinge and joined at a latch end by a releasable latch, so that the frame halves can be swung apart, placed around a pipe, and closed again. Three jaw carriages are distributed circumferentially about the closed frame. Each jaw carriage is slidable in a radial guide of the frame and carries a jaw pad that is mounted on the jaw carriage for pivoting movement and that presents a concave bearing surface to the pipe. Because each jaw pad pivots, it bears on the pipe without requiring a tool specific pipe diameter.
[0012] A cam ring is retained in an annular guide of the frame. The cam ring is segmented. A first cam-ring segment is carried by the first frame half and a second cam-ring segment is carried by the second frame half, so each segment stays with its own frame half when the clamp is opened. The first cam-ring segment and the second cam-ring segment have complementary drive faces at the hinge end and complementary drive faces at the latch end. Closing the frame halves brings those complementary drive faces into engagement and couples the cam-ring segments so that they move together as one cam ring about the pipe.
[0013] Each jaw carriage carries a follower that is received in a respective oblique slot of the cam ring. The latch carries a wedge. Moving the latch drives the wedge against a ring drive face of the cam ring and rotates the coupled cam ring through a limited stroke about the pipe. Each oblique slot converts that common rotation into inward travel of its own jaw carriage, so that all of the jaw carriages advance together through the same inward travel. A carriage spring biases each jaw carriage outward, so that releasing the latch retracts the jaw carriages and lifts the jaw pads off the pipe before the frame halves are opened.
[0014] The latch has a first position in which it holds the frame halves around the pipe without clamping and a second, over-center position in which it drives the cam ring and clamps the pipe. The two positions separate the act of capturing the pipe from the act of loading the jaws.
[0015] A force-limiting spring stack lies in the drive path between a latch handle of the latch and the wedge. Once all of the jaw pads have contacted the pipe, further movement of the latch handle toward the over-center position compresses the force-limiting spring stack rather than forcing one jaw pad farther than the others. The latch travel that remains after contact is therefore taken up by the force-limiting spring stack.
[0016] An indicator pin is visible on the latch and moves relative to a fixed edge of a latch housing of the latch when the force-limiting spring stack enters a predetermined compression range, giving the operator a mechanical report that the clamp is loaded within that range. The indicator reports clamp loading and does not measure pipe diameter.
[0017] The frame may carry a mounting boss outside the annular guide for supporting a sensor, a tool or a conduit. The mounting boss does not participate in synchronizing the jaw carriages.
[0018] A method of clamping a pipe and a method of releasing the clamp are also disclosed, in which the frame halves are closed about the pipe to couple the cam-ring segments, the latch is moved to the first position and then to the over-center position to rotate the coupled cam ring, and the latch travel that remains after the jaw pads seat is taken up by the force-limiting spring stack.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

[0023] In the following description, like reference numerals designate like parts throughout the several views. The described arrangement is one form of the invention and is not a limitation on the scope of the claims. Terms of orientation such as inward, outward, radial and circumferential are used with reference to a central axis of the closed frame, which in use is substantially coincident with the axis of the pipe being gripped.
[0024] Overall arrangement
[0025] Referring to FIG. 1, a split pipe clamp 10 comprises a first frame half 12 and a second frame half 14. The first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 are each substantially semicircular, and together they define a frame that surrounds a pipe 20 when closed. The first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 are joined at a hinge end of the frame by a hinge 16 and are releasably joined at a latch end of the frame, opposite the hinge end, by a releasable latch 18. Swinging the first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 apart about the hinge 16 opens the frame laterally, so that the split pipe clamp 10 can be placed around the pipe 20. The split pipe clamp 10 and the pipe 20 together form an installed clamp assembly when the split pipe clamp 10 is closed and clamped on the pipe 20.
[0026] Three jaw carriages 22 are distributed circumferentially about the closed frame, as shown in FIG. 1.
[0027] The frame carries a mounting boss 66. The mounting boss 66 supports a sensor, a tool or a conduit on the split pipe clamp 10. The mounting boss 66 does not participate in synchronizing the jaw carriages 22.
[0028] Jaw carriages and jaw pads
[0029] Referring to FIG. 2, each jaw carriage 22 is slidable in a respective radial guide 24 of the frame. The radial guide 24 constrains the jaw carriage 22 to travel along a radial path toward and away from the central axis of the closed frame.
[0030] Each jaw carriage 22 carries a jaw pad 26 that is mounted on the jaw carriage 22 for pivoting movement and that presents a concave bearing surface toward the central axis of the closed frame. Because the jaw pad 26 pivots relative to the jaw carriage 22, the jaw pad 26 bears on the pipe 20 without requiring a tool specific pipe diameter.
[0031] A carriage spring 42 biases each jaw carriage 22 outward, away from the central axis of the closed frame. When the releasable latch 18 is released, the carriage springs 42 retract the jaw carriages 22 outward and lift the jaw pads 26 off the pipe 20 before the frame halves are opened.
[0032] The segmented cam ring
[0033] A cam ring 30 is retained in an annular guide 36 of the frame. The annular guide 36, shown in FIG. 2, retains the cam ring 30 while allowing the cam ring 30 to rotate through a limited stroke about the central axis of the closed frame.
[0034] The mounting boss 66, shown in FIG. 1, stands on the frame outside the annular guide 36.
[0035] The cam ring 30 is segmented. Referring to FIG. 3, the cam ring 30 comprises a first cam-ring segment 32 and a second cam-ring segment 34. The first cam-ring segment 32 is carried by the first frame half 12 and the second cam-ring segment 34 is carried by the second frame half 14, each segment being retained by the frame half that carries it. Each segment accordingly stays with its own frame half when the frame is opened, and the frame halves can be swung apart without dismantling the drive.
[0036] The first cam-ring segment 32 has a first hinge-end drive face 44 and a first latch-end drive face 48. The second cam-ring segment 34 has a second hinge-end drive face 46 and a second latch-end drive face 50. The first hinge-end drive face 44 and the second hinge-end drive face 46 are complementary to one another, and the first latch-end drive face 48 and the second latch-end drive face 50 are complementary to one another. Closing the first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 brings the first hinge-end drive face 44 into engagement with the second hinge-end drive face 46 at the hinge end and brings the first latch-end drive face 48 into engagement with the second latch-end drive face 50 at the latch end, and thereby couples the first cam-ring segment 32 and the second cam-ring segment 34 so that they rotate together as one cam ring 30 about the central axis of the closed frame.
[0037] In the closed frame each pair of complementary drive faces transmits circumferential drive from one cam-ring segment to the other, and the complementary drive faces separate when the first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 are swung apart about the hinge 16.
[0038] Each jaw carriage 22 carries a follower 40 that is received in a respective oblique slot 38 of the cam ring 30. The follower 40 and the oblique slot 38 are shown in FIG. 2. Each oblique slot 38 is inclined to the circumferential direction of the cam ring 30, so that rotation of the cam ring 30 through the limited stroke displaces the follower 40, and with it the jaw carriage 22, along the radial guide 24. The oblique slots 38 are arranged so that rotation of the coupled cam ring 30 through the limited stroke produces the same inward travel at every jaw carriage 22. The inward travel of the three jaw carriages 22 is thereby synchronized.
[0039] The latch drive
[0040] The releasable latch 18 carries a wedge 52, as shown in FIG. 4. Moving the releasable latch 18 drives the wedge 52 against a ring drive face 54 of the cam ring 30, shown in FIG. 3, and rotates the coupled cam ring 30 through the limited stroke.
[0041] The releasable latch 18 has a first position and a second position. In the first position the releasable latch 18 holds the first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 around the pipe 20 without clamping the pipe 20. The second position is an over-center position, in which the releasable latch 18 has driven the wedge 52 and the cam ring 30 and clamps the pipe 20.
[0042] Referring again to FIG. 4, the releasable latch 18 comprises a latch handle 56, a latch housing 58 and a force-limiting spring stack 62. The force-limiting spring stack 62 lies in a drive path between the latch handle 56 and the wedge 52.
[0043] Once all of the jaw pads 26 have contacted the pipe 20, further movement of the latch handle 56 toward the over-center position compresses the force-limiting spring stack 62 rather than forcing one jaw pad 26 farther than the others. The latch travel that remains after contact is therefore taken up by the force-limiting spring stack 62.
[0044] An indicator pin 64 is visible on the releasable latch 18 and moves relative to a fixed edge 60 of the latch housing 58 when the force-limiting spring stack 62 enters a predetermined compression range. The position of the indicator pin 64 relative to the fixed edge 60 is a mechanical report that the split pipe clamp 10 is loaded within that range. The indicator pin 64 reports clamp loading and does not measure the diameter of the pipe 20.
[0045] Installation and removal
[0046] To install the split pipe clamp 10, an operator opens the releasable latch 18 and swings the first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 apart about the hinge 16. The operator places the frame halves around the pipe 20 and closes them, which brings the first hinge-end drive face 44 into engagement with the second hinge-end drive face 46 and the first latch-end drive face 48 into engagement with the second latch-end drive face 50, and so couples the first cam-ring segment 32 and the second cam-ring segment 34 into a single cam ring 30. The operator then moves the releasable latch 18 to the first position, in which the frame halves are held around the pipe 20 without clamping. The operator then moves the releasable latch 18 to the over-center position. The wedge 52 bears on the ring drive face 54 and rotates the coupled cam ring 30 through the limited stroke, the oblique slots 38 advance all of the jaw carriages 22 inward together, and the jaw pads 26 pivot and seat on the pipe 20. Latch travel remaining after the jaw pads 26 seat compresses the force-limiting spring stack 62, and the operator observes the indicator pin 64 relative to the fixed edge 60.
[0047] To remove the split pipe clamp 10, the operator releases the releasable latch 18. The force-limiting spring stack 62 unloads and the carriage springs 42 retract the jaw carriages 22, lifting the jaw pads 26 off the pipe 20 before the frame halves are opened. The first frame half 12 and the second frame half 14 are then swung apart about the hinge 16, and the first hinge-end drive face 44, the second hinge-end drive face 46, the first latch-end drive face 48 and the second latch-end drive face 50 separate so that the first cam-ring segment 32 and the second cam-ring segment 34 travel with their own frame halves. The split pipe clamp 10 is reusable.
[0048] The essential relationship of the arrangement described here is that closure of the frame halves couples the cam-ring segments, that travel of the releasable latch 18 drives the coupled cam ring 30, that the oblique slots 38 synchronize the radial travel of the jaw carriages 22, and that the force-limiting spring stack 62 accommodates the remaining latch travel. The features described above may be combined in ways other than those expressly set out, and modifications within the understanding of a person skilled in the art fall within the scope of the claims that follow.

ABSTRACT OF THE DISCLOSURE

A reusable split pipe clamp has two frame halves joined by a hinge at one end and a releasable latch at the other, so the frame opens around a pipe already in place. Jaw carriages slide in radial guides and carry pivoting concave jaw pads. A segmented cam ring sits in an annular guide, one segment traveling with each frame half, and complementary drive faces at the hinge end and the latch end couple the segments into one rotating ring when the frame closes. A wedge on the latch turns the coupled ring through a limited stroke against a ring drive face, and oblique slots acting on followers advance the jaw carriages inward together. A force-limiting spring stack between the latch handle and the wedge absorbs remaining latch travel, and an indicator pin moving relative to a fixed edge of the latch housing reports clamping load.
