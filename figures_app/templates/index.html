Compile the figures of a patent

Give it a patent as a PDF or as a link, and it builds the figures from the description: the components, their reference numerals and the relationships the text actually states. It never traces the drawings that were filed. Those are shown beside the generated ones so you can compare.

Google Patents and Espacenet links are recognised, as is a bare publication number. The link itself is never fetched: only the publication number is read out of it.

Reference-guided looks up the patents the examiner cited against this one, shows their drawings to an image model as an example of the idiom, and asks for this patent's arrangement in that style. The artwork is generated; the reference numerals are not — they are placed by the same code that places them on a schematic, and a sheet the model writes any text on is thrown away and redrawn. It needs a publication number, and any figure it cannot draw or ground falls back to the schematic.

PDF, .docx, .txt or .md, up to 40 MB. A scanned PDF with no text layer cannot be used: the compiler builds from words, not pixels.

What it will and will not do

It will

  • Read the description, the brief description of the drawings and the claims, and build a reference-numeral registry from them.
  • Recreate exactly the figure set the patent describes, with its numbering.
  • Draw block diagrams, data-flow and logical figures, flowcharts of disclosed methods, and conservative mechanical schematics.
  • Render every line, numeral, arrow and leader programmatically, then check the finished sheet against the patent and have it read back by a model that has not seen the specification.
  • Block a figure, and say why, when the draft is contradictory or underspecified.

It will not

  • Ask an image model to draw anything. No numeral on any sheet came from a generative model.
  • Trace, copy or read geometry from the drawings that were filed.
  • Invent a reference numeral. A part the description leaves unnumbered comes back as needs text update, naming the part.
  • Guess what a component looks like from its name. Where the text discloses no shape, the drawing is an honest rectangle.
  • Draw two embodiments as though they held at once.
